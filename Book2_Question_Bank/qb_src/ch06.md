# Chapter 6 — Adipose Tissue

**Q1.** The unilocular white adipocyte is recognised by:
A) Multiple small vacuoles and a central nucleus
B) A single large vacuole with a flattened peripheral nucleus
C) Eosinophilic granular cytoplasm
D) A clock-face nucleus

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.1**
**Why B:** The signet-ring profile results from lipid extraction during processing.
**Why not:** A) Brown fat. C) Mitochondria-rich cells. D) Plasma cell.

**Q2.** Brown adipose tissue generates heat through:
A) ATP synthase  B) Shivering  C) UCP1 (thermogenin) uncoupling oxidation from phosphorylation  D) Glycolysis

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.3**
**Why C:** UCP1 allows protons to bypass ATP synthase, dissipating the gradient as heat.
**Why not:** A) Captures energy as ATP. B) Muscle mechanism. D) Not the thermogenic pathway here.

**Q3.** Brown fat is brown because of:
A) Melanin  B) Lipofuscin  C) Haemosiderin  D) Abundant mitochondrial cytochromes and rich vascularity

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.3**
**Why D:** Dense mitochondria plus capillaries give the colour.
**Why not:** A, B, C are other pigments not responsible.

**Q4.** Leptin acts principally on the:
A) Hypothalamic arcuate nucleus to suppress appetite  B) Liver  C) Pancreas  D) Adrenal cortex

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.4**
**Why A:** It is the afferent signal reporting fat mass to the brain.
**Why not:** B, C, D are not its primary satiety target.

**Q5.** Common human obesity is characterised by:
A) Leptin deficiency  B) High leptin with leptin resistance  C) Absent leptin receptors universally  D) Low adipocyte number

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.4**
**Why B:** This is why leptin therapy is ineffective in ordinary obesity but curative in congenital deficiency.
**Why not:** A) Rare monogenic form. C) Not universal. D) Adipocyte number is increased or normal.

**Q6.** Which adipokine falls as adiposity increases and improves insulin sensitivity?
A) Leptin  B) TNF-α  C) Adiponectin  D) Resistin

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.4**
**Why C:** Low adiponectin is a marker of metabolic risk — the inverse relationship is the distinguishing feature.
**Why not:** A, B, D all rise with adiposity.

**Q7.** Crown-like structures in adipose tissue represent:
A) Multinucleate giant cells of granuloma
B) Capillary proliferation
C) Lipoblasts
D) Rings of macrophages surrounding a dying adipocyte

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 6.4**
**Why D:** A direct histological demonstration that obesity is a chronic inflammatory state.
**Why not:** A) Different context and morphology. C) Liposarcoma. B) Not annular.

**Q8.** A vacuolated cell whose nucleus is indented and hyperchromatic, within a soft tissue tumour, is:
A) A lipoblast, indicating liposarcoma  B) A normal adipocyte  C) A macrophage  D) A signet-ring carcinoma cell

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 6.5**
**Why A:** Scalloping/indentation of an atypical nucleus by vacuoles is the decisive malignant feature.
**Why not:** B) Smoothly compressed bland nucleus. C) No large lipid vacuole. D) Vacuole is mucin, PAS/mucicarmine positive.

**Q9.** Signet-ring cell carcinoma is distinguished from adipocytes by:
A) Nuclear position  B) Intracytoplasmic mucin positive with PAS/mucicarmine and nuclear atypia  C) Cell size  D) Vacuole number

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.5**
**Why B:** The content of the vacuole and nuclear features settle it.
**Why not:** A) Both may be peripheral. C, D) Unreliable.

**Q10.** Lipodystrophy causes severe insulin resistance because:
A) There is too much adipose tissue
B) Leptin is overproduced
C) Absent adipose storage capacity forces ectopic lipid deposition in liver and muscle
D) Adipocytes are inflamed

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 6.4**
**Why C:** It demonstrates that failure of *appropriate* fat storage, not fat itself, drives metabolic disease.
**Why not:** A) The opposite. B) Leptin is low. D) Not the main mechanism.

**Q11.** Brown adipose tissue in the newborn is concentrated:
A) In the abdomen only
B) Subcutaneously and uniformly
C) In the limbs
D) Interscapular, perirenal, and around the great vessels

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.3**
**Why D:** Sited to warm blood returning to the core.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect distributions.

**Q12.** Beige (brite) adipocytes:
A) Appear within white depots in response to cold or β-adrenergic stimulation
B) Never express UCP1
C) Are found only in infants
D) Are identical to white adipocytes

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 6.3**
**Why A:** "Browning" is a recognised, potentially therapeutic adaptive response.
**Why not:** B) They do. C) Present in adults. D) Functionally distinct.

**Q13.** The statement "brown fat disappears completely after infancy" is:
A) Correct
B) Incorrect — metabolically active depots are detectable in adults by PET-CT
C) Correct only in males
D) Correct only in cold climates

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.3**
**Why B:** Supraclavicular and paravertebral depots persist.
**Why not:** A, C, D perpetuate the outdated claim.

**Q14.** The sympathetic receptor mediating brown fat thermogenesis is:
A) α1  B) M3  C) β3  D) Nicotinic

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.3**
**Why C:** Noradrenaline at β3 receptors drives lipolysis and UCP1 activity.
**Why not:** A, B, D mediate other responses.

**Q15.** Adipose tissue is supported by a delicate network of:
A) Type I collagen bundles  B) Keratin  C) Elastic laminae  D) Reticular (type III) fibres

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.1**
**Why D:** Fine reticular fibres surround each adipocyte with abundant capillaries.
**Why not:** A) Too coarse. B, C) Not the supporting stroma here.

**Q16.** In H&E, the thin rim of adipocyte cytoplasm appears as:
A) A barely visible eosinophilic line around a clear space  B) A thick basophilic band  C) Granular brown material  D) Nothing at all

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.1**
**Why A:** Almost the entire cell volume is occupied by extracted lipid.
**Why not:** B, C) Incorrect appearance. D) B rim is discernible.

**Q17.** Lipomas are:
A) Malignant  B) The commonest benign soft-tissue tumour, composed of mature adipocytes  C) Composed of brown fat  D) Always multiple

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.5**
**Why B:** Encapsulated, histologically near-normal fat.
**Why not:** A) Benign. C) Hibernoma is the brown-fat tumour. D) Usually solitary.

**Q18.** Sebaceous gland cells may be confused with adipocytes, but are distinguished by:
A) Peripheral nucleus  B) Larger size  C) Multivacuolated cytoplasm, lobular arrangement around a follicle, and holocrine disintegration  D) Lack of vacuoles

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.2**
**Why C:** Architecture and the dying cells toward the duct are decisive.
**Why not:** A) Adipocyte feature. B, D) Not discriminating.

**Q19.** White adipose tissue contributes to thermal insulation and mechanical cushioning, and additionally functions as:
A) A purely inert store  B) A lymphoid organ  C) A haemopoietic organ in adults  D) The largest endocrine organ by mass

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.4**
**Why D:** Adipokine secretion makes it an endocrine tissue.
**Why not:** A) Obsolete view. C) Yellow marrow is fat but not haemopoietic. B) Incorrect.

**Q20.** TNF-α and IL-6 from adipose tissue contribute to insulin resistance by:
A) Impairing insulin receptor signalling
B) Destroying beta cells
C) Blocking glucose absorption
D) Increasing leptin clearance

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.4**
**Why A:** Chronic low-grade inflammation interferes with post-receptor signalling.
**Why not:** B) Type 1 mechanism. C, D) Not the pathway.

**Q21.** Adipocyte mitochondria in brown fat differ from white fat by being:
A) Fewer and smaller  B) Numerous, large, with densely packed cristae  C) Absent  D) Tubular-cristae type

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.3**
**Why B:** The thermogenic machinery requires abundant mitochondria.
**Why not:** A) Describes white fat. C) False. D) Steroidogenic cells.

**Q22.** Fat necrosis following trauma or pancreatitis is characterised histologically by:
A) Caseous debris  B) Fibrinoid change  C) Saponification — chalky calcium soaps with ghost adipocytes  D) Liquefaction only

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 6.5**
**Why C:** Released lipase liberates fatty acids that chelate calcium.
**Why not:** A) TB. B) Vasculitis. D) Brain/abscess.

**Q23.** The main energy-storage molecule in the adipocyte is:
A) Glycogen  B) Phospholipid  C) Cholesterol ester  D) Triglyceride

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 6.1**
**Why D:** Triglyceride yields more energy per gram than carbohydrate and is stored anhydrously.
**Why not:** A) Liver/muscle store. C) Steroidogenic precursor pool. B) Membrane component.

**Q24.** Hormone-sensitive lipase in adipocytes is activated by:
A) Catecholamines and glucagon  B) Insulin  C) Leptin  D) Adiponectin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.4**
**Why A:** Fasting and stress hormones mobilise stored lipid; insulin is antilipolytic.
**Why not:** B) Opposes lipolysis. C, D) Not direct activators.

**Q25.** A section shows lobules of cells with many small vacuoles, round central nuclei, and abundant capillaries, from a neonate's interscapular region. This is:
A) White adipose tissue  B) Brown adipose tissue  C) Sebaceous gland  D) Liposarcoma

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 6.2**
**Why B:** Multilocular morphology, central nuclei, site and age are all concordant.
**Why not:** A) Unilocular with peripheral nuclei. C) Attached to a follicle with a duct. D) Atypical lipoblasts.
