# Chapter 11 — Immune System and Lymphoid Organs

**Q1.** The decisive histological feature of the thymus is the:
A) Germinal centre  B) Subcapsular sinus  C) Hassall's corpuscle  D) Central arteriole

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 11.3**
**Why C:** Whorled keratinised medullary epithelial cells found in no other organ.
**Why not:** A) Secondary lymphoid organs. B) Lymph node. D) Spleen.

**Q2.** The thymus lacks afferent lymphatics because it:
A) Is too small
B) Is not a lymphoid organ
C) Has no blood supply
D) Must exclude foreign antigen while educating T cells against self

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.3**
**Why D:** Architecture follows the antigen's route; here antigen must be kept out.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q3.** Positive selection of thymocytes occurs in the ____ and selects for ____:
A) Cortex; ability to recognise self-MHC  B) Medulla; low self-affinity  C) Cortex; strong self-peptide binding  D) Medulla; MHC restriction

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.3**
**Why A:** Cells that cannot recognise self-MHC die by neglect; this establishes MHC restriction.
**Why not:** B, D) Negative selection occurs in the medulla. C) That triggers deletion.

**Q4.** AIRE enables medullary thymic epithelial cells to:
A) Produce antibodies
B) Express tissue-specific self-antigens for negative selection
C) Phagocytose thymocytes
D) Secrete thymosin

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.3**
**Why B:** Its mutation causes APECED/APS-1, proving negative selection prevents autoimmunity.
**Why not:** A, C, D are other functions or cells.

**Q5.** Germinal centres are found in:
A) Primary lymphoid organs  B) The thymic cortex normally  C) Secondary lymphoid organs following antigen exposure  D) Bone marrow

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.2**
**Why C:** They are sites of somatic hypermutation, affinity maturation and class switching.
**Why not:** A, B, D) Their presence in the thymus is abnormal (e.g. myasthenia gravis).

**Q6.** High endothelial venules are located in the lymph node:
A) Subcapsular sinus  B) Medullary sinus  C) Germinal centre  D) Paracortex

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.4**
**Why D:** Their plump cuboidal endothelium allows circulating lymphocytes to enter the node from blood.
**Why not:** A, B, C do not contain HEVs.

**Q7.** The paracortex of a lymph node is populated chiefly by:
A) T cells  B) B cells  C) Plasma cells  D) Macrophages only

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 11.4**
**Why A:** Follicles (cortex) are B-cell zones; medullary cords contain plasma cells.
**Why not:** B) Cortex. C) Medulla. D) Sinuses.

**Q8.** Marked paracortical expansion in a reactive lymph node suggests:
A) Bacterial infection with a humoral response
B) A viral infection or drug reaction (T-cell response)
C) Metastatic carcinoma
D) Amyloidosis

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.4**
**Why B:** Reading the *pattern* of reaction reveals the type of immune response.
**Why not:** A) Produces follicular hyperplasia. C) Subcapsular sinus deposits. D) Congo red positive deposits.

**Q9.** Florid follicular hyperplasia indicates a predominantly:
A) T-cell response  B) Granulomatous response  C) B-cell/humoral response  D) Neoplastic process

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.4**
**Why C:** Enlarged germinal centres reflect B-cell activation.
**Why not:** A) Paracortical. B) Granulomas. D) Not necessarily.

**Q10.** The spleen's white pulp is recognised by the presence of:
A) A subcapsular sinus  B) Villi  C) Hassall's corpuscles  D) An eccentric central arteriole within the lymphoid nodule

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.5**
**Why D:** A lymph node follicle never contains an arteriole.
**Why not:** A) Lymph node. C) Thymus. B) Intestine.

**Q11.** The periarteriolar lymphoid sheath (PALS) contains mainly:
A) T cells  B) B cells  C) Plasma cells  D) Erythrocytes

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.5**
**Why A:** B-cell follicles lie eccentrically alongside the PALS.
**Why not:** B) Follicles. C) Cords. D) Red pulp.

**Q12.** The open circulation of the spleen is functionally essential because it:
A) Speeds blood flow
B) Forces red cells to squeeze between stave cells, filtering out rigid or abnormal cells
C) Prevents clotting
D) Oxygenates the blood

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.5**
**Why B:** This mechanical test underlies splenomegaly in haemolytic disease and the benefit of splenectomy.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q13.** Post-splenectomy patients are at risk of overwhelming infection with:
A) Viruses only
B) Fungi only
C) Encapsulated bacteria — pneumococcus, Hib, meningococcus
D) Helminths

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.5**
**Why C:** The spleen is the principal site of antibody responses to polysaccharide capsular antigens.
**Why not:** A, B, D are not the characteristic risk.

**Q14.** The cords of Billroth are located in the:
A) White pulp  B) Capsule  C) Marginal zone  D) Red pulp

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.5**
**Why D:** A reticular meshwork with macrophages between sinusoids.
**Why not:** A, C) Lymphoid compartments. B) Fibrous covering.

**Q15.** M cells of Peyer's patches function to:
A) Transcytose luminal antigen to underlying immune cells
B) Secrete IgA
C) Absorb nutrients
D) Produce mucus

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.6**
**Why A:** Their basolateral pocket holds lymphocytes and dendritic cells.
**Why not:** B) Plasma cells. C) Enterocytes. D) Goblet cells.

**Q16.** Salmonella and Shigella exploit M cells because these cells:
A) Lack a membrane  B) Provide a transcytotic portal across the epithelium  C) Secrete nutrients  D) Are immunodeficient

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.6**
**Why B:** Any gateway can be subverted by pathogens.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q17.** Secretory IgA resists luminal proteolysis because of its:
A) Dimeric structure alone  B) J chain only  C) Retained secretory component  D) Glycosylation of the Fab region

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.6**
**Why C:** The cleaved remnant of the polymeric Ig receptor protects the molecule.
**Why not:** A, B) Necessary for transport but not for protection. D) Not the mechanism.

**Q18.** Tingible-body macrophages produce the "starry sky" appearance in germinal centres because they:
A) Are pigmented  B) Store iron  C) Are infected  D) Contain apoptotic debris of B cells failing selection

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.4**
**Why D:** They appear as pale cells against a dark background of proliferating lymphocytes.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q19.** The mantle zone of a secondary follicle consists of:
A) Compressed naive B cells displaced by the germinal centre  B) Proliferating blasts  C) T cells  D) Macrophages

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.4**
**Why A:** It is the remnant of the primary follicle.
**Why not:** B) Germinal centre. C) Paracortex. D) Sinuses.

**Q20.** In HIV infection, the lymph node characteristically shows:
A) Stable architecture
B) Progressive paracortical CD4+ depletion, with follicles first florid then involuting
C) Granulomas only
D) Fibrosis from the outset

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 11.4**
**Why B:** The node's histology tracks the course of the disease.
**Why not:** A, C, D do not describe the typical evolution.

**Q21.** Lymph flows through a node in the order:
A) Efferent → medullary → subcapsular → afferent
B) Afferent → medulla → cortex → efferent
C) Afferent → subcapsular sinus → cortical/trabecular sinuses → medullary sinuses → efferent
D) Hilum → capsule → cortex

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.4**
**Why C:** Multiple afferents enter the convex surface; a single efferent leaves at the hilum.
**Why not:** A, B, D misorder the flow.

**Q22.** Primary lymphoid organs are:
A) Lymph node and spleen  B) Spleen and thymus  C) Tonsil and appendix  D) Bone marrow and thymus

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 11.2**
**Why D:** Sites of lymphocyte generation and education without foreign antigen.
**Why not:** A, C) Secondary. B) Mixed.

**Q23.** Thymic involution after puberty is characterised by:
A) Replacement of parenchyma by adipose tissue, with Hassall's corpuscles persisting
B) Complete disappearance
C) Fibrosis with calcification
D) Conversion to lymph node

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.3**
**Why A:** This is how an adult thymus remains identifiable.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q24.** The palatine tonsil is distinguished from a lymph node by:
A) Having a complete capsule and sinuses
B) Being covered by stratified squamous epithelium with deep branching crypts and lacking afferent lymphatics
C) Containing Hassall's corpuscles
D) Having a central arteriole

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.6**
**Why B:** MALT is unencapsulated and directly beneath an epithelium.
**Why not:** A) Lymph node. C) Thymus. D) Spleen.

**Q25.** A section shows lymphoid follicles beneath an intestinal epithelium containing villi, with no capsule. This is:
A) Lymph node  B) Spleen  C) Peyer's patch  D) Thymus

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 11.6**
**Why C:** Unencapsulated MALT in the ileal mucosa/submucosa with M cells in the overlying epithelium.
**Why not:** A) Encapsulated with sinuses. B) Central arterioles and red pulp. D) Hassall's corpuscles.
