# Chapter 4 — Epithelial Tissue

**Q1.** The decisive criterion distinguishing pseudostratified from stratified epithelium is:
A) Number of nuclear rows  B) Presence of cilia  C) Cell shape at the surface  D) Every cell contacts the basement membrane

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.2**
**Why D:** Pseudostratification is a single layer with staggered nuclei.
**Why not:** A) Staggered nuclei create the illusion. B) Common but not definitional. C) Used for classifying true stratified epithelia.

**Q2.** Epithelia are classified by the shape of:
A) Surface cells  B) Basal cells  C) The largest cells  D) The nuclei

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.2**
**Why A:** Stratified squamous has cuboidal basal cells; the surface layer names it.
**Why not:** B, C, D would misclassify most stratified epithelia.

**Q3.** Urothelium (transitional epithelium) is identified by:
A) Keratin  B) Large dome-shaped, often binucleate umbrella cells  C) Goblet cells  D) Cilia

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.3**
**Why B:** Umbrella cells with specialised plaques allow distension and provide an osmotic barrier.
**Why not:** A) Urothelium is non-keratinised. C, D) Belong to respiratory/intestinal epithelia.

**Q4.** In pemphigus vulgaris the autoantibody targets desmoglein 3, producing:
A) A subepidermal blister  B) Dermal fibrosis  C) An intraepidermal blister with acantholysis  D) Basement membrane thickening

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.4**
**Why C:** Desmosomes join keratinocytes to each other, so the split is within the epidermis and blisters are flaccid.
**Why not:** A) That is bullous pemphigoid (hemidesmosome). B, D) Not blistering mechanisms.

**Q5.** Bullous pemphigoid produces a tense, subepidermal blister because the antigens are:
A) Desmoglein 1 and 3  B) E-cadherin  C) Type IV collagen  D) BP180 and BP230 of the hemidesmosome

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.4**
**Why D:** Hemidesmosomes anchor basal cells to the basement membrane, so the whole epidermis lifts off.
**Why not:** A) Pemphigus. C) Alport/Goodpasture. B) Adherens junction.

**Q6.** Loss of E-cadherin explains the discohesive, single-file growth of:
A) Lobular breast and diffuse gastric carcinoma  B) Squamous cell carcinoma  C) Basal cell carcinoma  D) Melanoma

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 4.4**
**Why A:** CDH1 loss removes adherens-junction cohesion, so cells infiltrate singly rather than as nests.
**Why not:** B, C, D retain different adhesion profiles and growth patterns.

**Q7.** Familial hypomagnesaemia with hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis results from mutations in:
A) CLDN14  B) CLDN16 or CLDN19  C) Occludin  D) ZO-1

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 4.4**
**Why B:** Claudin-16/19 mediate paracellular Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ reabsorption in the thick ascending limb; CLDN19 disease adds ocular involvement.
**Why not:** A) CLDN14 is associated chiefly with hearing loss. C, D) Not the causative genes in FHHNC.

**Q8.** Tight junctions perform a "fence" function, meaning they:
A) Seal the paracellular space only
B) Anchor keratin filaments
C) Prevent mixing of apical and basolateral membrane proteins
D) Permit ionic coupling

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.4**
**Why C:** Restricting lateral diffusion within the membrane maintains polarity, enabling vectorial transport.
**Why not:** A) That is the separate "gate" function. B) Desmosomes/hemidesmosomes. D) Gap junctions.

**Q9.** Gap junction channels are formed by:
A) Claudins  B) Integrins  C) Cadherins  D) Connexins (six forming a connexon)

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.4**
**Why D:** Two apposed connexons form an aqueous channel for ions and small molecules.
**Why not:** A) Tight junctions. C) Adherens/desmosomes. B) Cell–matrix adhesion.

**Q10.** Connexin 26 mutations commonly cause:
A) Autosomal recessive deafness  B) Blistering  C) Nephrotic syndrome  D) Emphysema

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.4**
**Why A:** Cx26 is required for cochlear potassium recycling.
**Why not:** B) Junctional adhesion disorders. C) GBM disease. D) Protease imbalance.

**Q11.** The lamina densa of the basal lamina is composed principally of:
A) Type I collagen  B) Type IV collagen network with laminin, nidogen and perlecan  C) Elastin  D) Type VII collagen

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.5**
**Why B:** Type IV forms a sheet-like (not fibrillar) network, cross-linked with the laminin network.
**Why not:** A) Interstitial matrix. C) Elastic fibres. D) Anchoring fibrils below the lamina densa.

**Q12.** Goodpasture syndrome involves autoantibodies against:
A) Laminin-332
B) Desmoglein 3
C) The NC1 domain of the α3 chain of type IV collagen
D) Acetylcholine receptor

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 4.5**
**Why C:** The same α3 chain is present in glomerular and alveolar basement membranes, explaining the renal-pulmonary syndrome.
**Why not:** B) Pemphigus. A) Junctional EB. D) Myasthenia gravis.

**Q13.** Alport syndrome is most often inherited as:
A) Autosomal dominant  B) Autosomal recessive only  C) Mitochondrial  D) X-linked (COL4A5)

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.5**
**Why D:** COL4A5 mutations account for the majority; EM shows basket-weave splitting of the lamina densa.
**Why not:** A, B) Less common forms exist. C) Not mitochondrial.

**Q14.** The distinction between carcinoma in situ and invasive carcinoma rests on:
A) Breach of the basement membrane  B) Nuclear grade  C) Tumour size  D) Mitotic count

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.5**
**Why A:** Until the basement membrane is crossed, the tumour cannot access lymphatics or vessels and cannot metastasise.
**Why not:** B, C, D influence grade/stage but do not define invasion.

**Q15.** A brush border is most characteristic of:
A) Respiratory epithelium  B) Small intestinal absorptive cells and proximal renal tubule  C) Epidermis  D) Mesothelium

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.6**
**Why B:** Dense microvilli greatly amplify absorptive surface area.
**Why not:** A) Cilia and goblet cells. C) Keratin. D) Simple squamous.

**Q16.** Serous secretory cells are recognised by:
A) Pale foamy cytoplasm with a flattened basal nucleus
B) Empty vacuoles
C) Basophilic base, round nucleus, apical eosinophilic zymogen granules
D) Keratohyalin granules

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.7**
**Why C:** High RER for enzyme synthesis, with stored zymogen apically.
**Why not:** A) Mucous cells. B) Adipocytes. D) Epidermis.

**Q17.** Holocrine secretion is exemplified by:
A) Pancreatic acinar cells  B) Goblet cells  C) Lactating mammary gland lipid  D) Sebaceous glands

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.7**
**Why D:** The entire cell disintegrates and becomes the secretion.
**Why not:** A, B) Merocrine. C) Apocrine.

**Q18.** Serous demilunes are now regarded as:
A) Substantially a fixation artifact of mucous cell swelling, though the term remains in use
B) A purely native structure in all species
C) A form of metaplasia
D) Degenerating acini

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 4.7**
**Why A:** Cryofixation studies show intermingled serous and mucous cells without crescents, although the finding remains debated and the term is retained descriptively.
**Why not:** B) Overstates. C, D) Not pathological processes.

**Q19.** Epithelium is avascular, which means nutrients must:
A) Be synthesised locally
B) Diffuse from capillaries in the underlying connective tissue
C) Arrive via lymphatics within the epithelium
D) Come from the lumen only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.1**
**Why B:** This diffusion dependence limits epithelial thickness.
**Why not:** A, C) Epithelia contain no vessels or lymphatics. D) Not a general mechanism.

**Q20.** Simple squamous epithelium lining body cavities (pleura, peritoneum) is termed:
A) Endothelium  B) Urothelium  C) Mesothelium  D) Endometrium

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.3**
**Why C:** Endothelium lines vessels; mesothelium lines serous cavities.
**Why not:** A) Vascular. B) Urinary tract. D) A whole mucosa, not an epithelial class.

**Q21.** Anchoring fibrils that secure the lamina densa to underlying connective tissue are composed of:
A) Type I collagen  B) Fibrillin  C) Laminin  D) Type VII collagen

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.5**
**Why D:** Their defect causes dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa.
**Why not:** A) Interstitial. C) Within the lamina lucida/densa. B) Elastic system.

**Q22.** The type of epithelium in the trachea is:
A) Pseudostratified ciliated columnar with goblet cells  B) Simple columnar  C) Stratified squamous  D) Transitional

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 4.3**
**Why A:** Adapted for mucociliary clearance.
**Why not:** B) Found in gut. C) Oesophagus. D) Urinary tract.

**Q23.** In the glomerular basement membrane, charge selectivity is contributed mainly by:
A) Type IV collagen  B) Heparan sulfate proteoglycan (perlecan/agrin)  C) Nidogen  D) Fibronectin

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 4.5**
**Why B:** Its dense anionic charge repels negatively charged plasma proteins such as albumin.
**Why not:** A) Provides the size-selective scaffold. C) A bridging molecule. D) Not the principal charge barrier.

**Q24.** An epithelium showing nuclei at multiple levels, cilia, and goblet cells, in which careful inspection shows all cells reaching the base, is:
A) Stratified columnar  B) Transitional  C) Pseudostratified ciliated columnar  D) Stratified squamous

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.2**
**Why C:** The combination is essentially diagnostic of respiratory epithelium.
**Why not:** A) Rare, found in some large ducts, non-ciliated. B, D) Different surface morphology.

**Q25.** Which junction couples cardiac myocytes electrically, permitting the myocardium to act as a functional syncytium?
A) Desmosome  B) Hemidesmosome  C) Tight junction  D) Gap junction

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 4.4**
**Why D:** Connexin channels in the intercalated disc allow ionic current to spread cell-to-cell.
**Why not:** A) Provides mechanical adhesion in the same disc. B, C) Not present in that role.
