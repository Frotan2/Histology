# Chapter 7 — Nerve Tissue and Nervous System

**Q1.** Nissl substance corresponds ultrastructurally to:
A) Mitochondria  B) Lysosomes  C) Stacked rough ER and free ribosomes  D) Neurofilaments

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.1**
**Why C:** Its basophilia reflects the massive protein synthesis needed to maintain a long axon.
**Why not:** A) Eosinophilic. B, D) Not basophilic aggregates.

**Q2.** Nissl substance is absent from:
A) The perikaryon  B) Dendrites  C) The nucleus  D) The axon hillock and axon

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.1**
**Why D:** The axon has no ribosomes, which is why all axonal protein must be transported from the cell body.
**Why not:** A, B) Both contain Nissl substance. C) Not a cytoplasmic compartment.

**Q3.** One oligodendrocyte myelinates:
A) Segments of many different axons  B) A single internode of one axon  C) No axons  D) Only unmyelinated fibres

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.2**
**Why A:** Processes extend to up to ~50 axons — the key contrast with Schwann cells.
**Why not:** B) Schwann cell. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q4.** Peripheral nerves regenerate better than central axons largely because:
A) They are thicker
B) Schwann cells survive and form bands of Büngner guiding regrowth
C) They are unmyelinated
D) They lack connective tissue

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.2**
**Why B:** The CNS lacks this scaffold and has astrocytic scarring plus myelin-derived inhibitors.
**Why not:** A, C, D are not the determining factors.

**Q5.** Microglia differ from all other glia in being derived from:
A) Neuroectoderm  B) Neural crest  C) Mesoderm/yolk-sac macrophage progenitors  D) Endoderm

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.2**
**Why C:** They are the resident macrophages of the CNS — a classic examination trap.
**Why not:** A) All other glia. B) Schwann/satellite cells. D) Not a glial source.

**Q6.** Astrocytes are identified immunohistochemically by:
A) CD68  B) Cytokeratin  C) Desmin  D) GFAP

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.2**
**Why D:** Glial fibrillary acidic protein is their intermediate filament.
**Why not:** B) Epithelium. C) Muscle. A) Macrophages/microglia.

**Q7.** Saltatory conduction depends on concentration of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels at:
A) Nodes of Ranvier  B) Internodes  C) The soma  D) Dendritic spines

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.3**
**Why A:** Depolarisation regenerates only at nodes, so the impulse jumps between them.
**Why not:** B) Insulated by myelin. C, D) Not the propagation mechanism.

**Q8.** Myelin appears as a clear halo in routine H&E because:
A) It is basophilic  B) Its lipid is dissolved during processing  C) It is mineralised  D) It shrinks away

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.3**
**Why B:** Luxol fast blue or osmium is required to demonstrate it.
**Why not:** A) It is not stained. C) Not mineral. D) Not the principal reason.

**Q9.** The blood-nerve barrier resides in the:
A) Endoneurium  B) Epineurium  C) Perineurium, via tight junctions between its epithelioid cells  D) Schwann cell basal lamina

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.4**
**Why C:** Concentric perineurial layers with tight junctions surround each fascicle.
**Why not:** A) Loose tissue around individual axons. B) Outer dense sheath. D) Not a barrier layer.

**Q10.** The epineurium is:
A) Loose tissue around single axons
B) A myelin layer
C) A tight-junctional barrier
D) Dense irregular connective tissue binding fascicles, carrying the vasa nervorum

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.4**
**Why D:** It also contains adipose tissue.
**Why not:** A) Endoneurium. C) Perineurium. B) Schwann cell product.

**Q11.** Botulinum toxin causes flaccid paralysis by:
A) Cleaving SNARE proteins, preventing ACh release at the NMJ
B) Blocking acetylcholine receptors
C) Inhibiting acetylcholinesterase
D) Blocking glycine release

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.5**
**Why A:** No transmitter release means no muscle activation.
**Why not:** B) Curare-like agents. C) Would increase activity. D) Tetanus toxin.

**Q12.** Tetanus toxin causes spastic paralysis because it blocks release of:
A) Acetylcholine at the NMJ  B) Inhibitory glycine and GABA from spinal interneurons  C) Noradrenaline  D) Dopamine

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.5**
**Why B:** Loss of inhibition leaves motor neurons unopposed — the mirror image of botulism despite an identical molecular mechanism.
**Why not:** A) Botulinum. C, D) Not the target.

**Q13.** Myasthenia gravis results from autoantibodies against:
A) Presynaptic Ca²⁺ channels  B) MuSK only  C) Postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors  D) Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.5**
**Why C:** Produces fatigable weakness that improves with anticholinesterases. (Anti-MuSK is a recognised minority subtype.)
**Why not:** A) Lambert-Eaton. B) A subset, not the classic answer. D) Not a target.

**Q14.** Lambert-Eaton syndrome differs clinically from myasthenia gravis because strength:
A) Worsens with repeated use  B) Is lost abruptly  C) Is unaffected  D) Improves with repeated use

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.5**
**Why D:** Presynaptic Ca²⁺ channel antibodies reduce release; repetitive activity accumulates Ca²⁺ and facilitates it.
**Why not:** A) Myasthenia. B, C) Incorrect.

**Q15.** Multiple sclerosis plaques are characterised by:
A) Demyelination with relative axonal preservation, typically periventricular
B) Axonal loss with myelin preservation
C) Neuronal storage material
D) Amyloid plaques

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.6**
**Why A:** The insulation, not the wire, is the primary target.
**Why not:** B) Reversed. C) Storage diseases. D) Alzheimer disease.

**Q16.** Guillain-Barré syndrome is frequently preceded by infection with:
A) Staphylococcus aureus  B) Campylobacter jejuni  C) Candida  D) Influenza only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.6**
**Why B:** Molecular mimicry between bacterial lipo-oligosaccharide and peripheral nerve gangliosides.
**Why not:** A, C) Not classically associated. D) Occasionally implicated but not the classic answer.

**Q17.** Ependymal cells:
A) Myelinate CNS axons  B) Are phagocytic  C) Line the ventricles and central canal, ciliated, aiding CSF circulation  D) Form the blood-brain barrier

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.2**
**Why C:** Modified epithelium; choroid plexus epithelium produces CSF.
**Why not:** A) Oligodendrocytes. B) Microglia. D) Endothelial tight junctions with astrocytic induction.

**Q18.** A neuron with one axon and multiple dendrites is:
A) Unipolar  B) Bipolar  C) Pseudounipolar  D) Multipolar

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.1**
**Why D:** The commonest type — motor neurons and most CNS neurons.
**Why not:** B) Retina, olfactory. C) Dorsal root ganglion sensory neurons.

**Q19.** Dorsal root ganglion neurons are:
A) Pseudounipolar, surrounded by satellite cells  B) Multipolar  C) Bipolar  D) Anaxonic

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.1**
**Why A:** B single process bifurcates into peripheral and central branches; satellite cells encircle the somata.
**Why not:** B) Autonomic ganglia and CNS. C) Special sensory. D) Rare interneurons.

**Q20.** Wallerian degeneration refers to:
A) Degeneration of the proximal stump
B) Degeneration of the axon distal to injury, with myelin breakdown and macrophage clearance
C) Neuronal apoptosis
D) Demyelination without axonal injury

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.6**
**Why B:** The distal segment, separated from the perikaryon, cannot be maintained.
**Why not:** A) Limited retrograde change occurs but is not Wallerian. C, D) Different processes.

**Q21.** Chromatolysis in an injured neuron describes:
A) Nuclear fragmentation
B) Cytoplasmic calcification
C) Dispersal of Nissl substance with peripheral nuclear displacement and cell swelling
D) Myelin loss

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.6**
**Why C:** The reaction of the cell body to axonal injury, reflecting a shift to repair-oriented synthesis.
**Why not:** A) Karyorrhexis. B, D) Different phenomena.

**Q22.** The 20–30 nm gap at a chemical synapse is bridged by:
A) Gap junction channels  B) Tight junctions  C) Direct cytoplasmic continuity  D) Diffusing neurotransmitter

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.5**
**Why D:** Chemical transmission converts electrical to chemical signalling and back.
**Why not:** A, C) Electrical synapses. B) Not present.

**Q23.** On transverse section, a peripheral nerve is distinguished from tendon by:
A) Fascicles of small round haloed profiles surrounded by a distinct perineurial ring
B) Pink colour
C) Flattened nuclei in rows
D) Wavy parallel collagen

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.10**
**Why A:** The haloes are dissolved myelin around axons.
**Why not:** B) Both are pink. C, D) Both describe tendon.

**Q24.** Satellite cells in peripheral ganglia are functionally analogous to:
A) Microglia  B) Astrocytes/Schwann cells providing support and ionic regulation  C) Ependyma  D) Endothelium

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.2**
**Why B:** They encapsulate neuronal cell bodies and regulate their microenvironment.
**Why not:** A) Immune. C) Ventricular lining. D) Vascular.

**Q25.** Astrocyte end-feet contribute to the blood-brain barrier by:
A) Forming the tight junctions themselves
B) Phagocytosing pathogens
C) Inducing and maintaining endothelial tight junctions
D) Producing CSF

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.2**
**Why C:** The barrier resides in the endothelium, but astrocytes induce and sustain the phenotype — a frequently confused point.
**Why not:** A) Endothelial cells form them. B) Microglia. D) Choroid plexus.

**Q26.** The arrangement of grey and white matter is:
A) Grey outside in both brain and spinal cord
B) Grey inside in both
C) Grey outside in the cerebrum and cerebellum, but inside in the spinal cord
D) Randomly distributed

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.6**
**Why C:** Cortical grey lies superficially in brain; in the cord the grey forms a central butterfly surrounded by white funiculi. This inversion is the fastest orientation test on a CNS section.
**Why not:** A, B) Each is true of only one region. D) The arrangement is highly ordered.

**Q27.** A section shows a single row of very large flask-shaped neurons between an outer pale layer and an inner densely nucleated layer. This is:
A) Cerebral cortex
B) Dorsal root ganglion
C) Spinal cord grey matter
D) Cerebellar cortex, with Purkinje cells

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 7.7**
**Why D:** The single Purkinje cell row between molecular and granular layers is unique in the body.
**Why not:** A) Six indistinct layers with triangular pyramidal cells. C) Butterfly-shaped, no layering. B) Clustered round somata with satellite cells.

**Q28.** Purkinje cells are notable because they:
A) Are the sole output of the cerebellar cortex and are inhibitory (GABAergic)
B) Are the only excitatory output of the cerebellar cortex
C) Receive no input
D) Lie in the granular layer

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.7**
**Why A:** The entire cerebellar cortex computes onto one inhibitory output cell — a frequently examined point.
**Why not:** B) They are inhibitory, not excitatory. C) They receive parallel and climbing fibre input. D) They form their own layer.

**Q29.** An epidural haematoma is lens-shaped and arterial, whereas a subdural haematoma is crescent-shaped and venous, because:
A) Blood clots differently in each space
B) Dura is adherent to bone, so arterial blood must strip it away, while subdural venous blood spreads freely
C) The arachnoid is impermeable
D) CSF dilutes subdural blood

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 7.8**
**Why B:** The shape of each haemorrhage is a direct readout of meningeal anatomy and the vessel torn (middle meningeal artery versus bridging veins).
**Why not:** A) Clotting is the same. C, D) Neither determines the shape.

**Q30.** A ganglion shows clustered round neuronal somata, each fully encircled by satellite cells, with no synapses present. This is:
A) An autonomic ganglion
B) The choroid plexus
C) A dorsal root ganglion
D) A Meissner plexus

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 7.9**
**Why C:** Sensory information passes straight through a dorsal root ganglion, so no synapses occur there — the decisive difference from an autonomic ganglion.
**Why not:** A) Multipolar, scattered neurons *with* synapses and incomplete satellite rings. B) Cuboidal epithelium on vascular fronds. D) Enteric, within the gut wall.
