# Chapter 12 — Digestive System

**Q1.** Submucosal glands are found in only two regions of the digestive tract:
A) Stomach and colon  B) Ileum and appendix  C) Jejunum and ileum  D) Oesophagus and duodenum

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.1**
**Why D:** Oesophageal mucous glands and duodenal Brunner glands — one of the most reliable identification clues.
**Why not:** A, B, C have no submucosal glands.

**Q2.** The myenteric (Auerbach) plexus lies:
A) Between the inner circular and outer longitudinal muscle layers  B) In the lamina propria  C) In the submucosa  D) In the serosa

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.2**
**Why A:** Its position next to the muscle it governs reflects its role in motility.
**Why not:** B, C) Submucosal plexus territory. D) Not a plexus site.

**Q3.** The submucosal (Meissner) plexus principally controls:
A) Motility  B) Secretion and local blood flow  C) Swallowing  D) Vomiting

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.2**
**Why B:** Position predicts function — it lies adjacent to the mucosa.
**Why not:** A) Myenteric plexus. C, D) Central reflexes.

**Q4.** In Hirschsprung disease, the diseased segment is:
A) The dilated proximal segment  B) The entire colon always  C) The narrow distal aganglionic segment  D) The ileum

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 12.2**
**Why C:** The aganglionic segment remains tonically contracted; proximal dilatation is secondary — a classic trap.
**Why not:** A) Secondary change. B, D) Usually rectosigmoid.

**Q5.** The definitive diagnosis of Hirschsprung disease rests on:
A) Barium enema alone
B) Manometry only
C) Serum markers
D) Rectal suction biopsy showing absent ganglion cells with hypertrophied nerve trunks

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.2**
**Why D:** Histology is definitive.
**Why not:** A, B, C are supportive at best.

**Q6.** Parietal cells are recognised by:
A) Large round/pyramidal shape with intensely eosinophilic cytoplasm and a central round nucleus
B) Basophilic basal cytoplasm with apical granules
C) Pale foamy cytoplasm
D) Flattened basal nucleus

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.3**
**Why A:** Their abundant mitochondria provide ATP for proton pumping.
**Why not:** B) Chief cells. C, D) Mucous cells.

**Q7.** Parietal cells secrete:
A) Pepsinogen and lipase  B) HCl and intrinsic factor  C) Gastrin  D) Mucus and bicarbonate

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.3**
**Why B:** One cell type, two products — and two clinical syndromes if lost.
**Why not:** A) Chief cells. C) G cells. D) Surface mucous cells.

**Q8.** Autoimmune destruction of parietal cells causes:
A) Iron deficiency only
B) Coeliac disease
C) Achlorhydria plus B12 deficiency (pernicious anaemia)
D) Peptic ulcer

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.3**
**Why C:** Loss of intrinsic factor impairs ileal B12 absorption, causing megaloblastic anaemia and subacute combined degeneration.
**Why not:** A) A secondary consequence at most. B, D) Different mechanisms.

**Q9.** The three converging stimuli for gastric acid secretion are:
A) Secretin, CCK, VIP  B) Insulin, glucagon, GIP  C) Somatostatin, gastrin, motilin  D) Gastrin, histamine, acetylcholine

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.3**
**Why D:** This is why PPIs, acting on the final common effector, outperform H2 blockers.
**Why not:** A, B, C are not the acid-secretory triad.

**Q10.** Tubulovesicles in the resting parietal cell contain:
A) H⁺/K⁺-ATPase awaiting insertion into the canalicular membrane  B) Pepsinogen  C) Mucin  D) Intrinsic factor only

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 12.3**
**Why A:** On stimulation they fuse with the canaliculus, expanding surface area and inserting pumps.
**Why not:** B) Chief cells. C) Mucous cells. D) Secreted separately.

**Q11.** Brunner glands are located in the:
A) Lamina propria of the jejunum  B) Submucosa of the duodenum  C) Muscularis of the stomach  D) Mucosa of the ileum

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.4**
**Why B:** They secrete alkaline mucus neutralising acidic chyme — position, function and identification coincide.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q12.** Peyer's patches are most prominent in the:
A) Duodenum  B) Jejunum  C) Ileum  D) Colon

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.4**
**Why C:** Lymphoid aggregates spanning mucosa into submucosa are the decisive ileal feature.
**Why not:** A, B) Absent or sparse. D) Different lymphoid pattern.

**Q13.** The decisive difference between colon and small intestine on section is:
A) Goblet cell number  B) Epithelial type  C) Presence of a muscularis mucosae  D) Absence of villi in the colon

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.5**
**Why D:** Colonic mucosa presents a flat surface pierced by straight crypts.
**Why not:** A) Suggestive but not decisive. C) Both have one. B) Both are simple columnar.

**Q14.** Villi are distinguished from obliquely cut crypts by the fact that villi:
A) Project above the surface and contain a lamina propria core with a central lacteal
B) Contain goblet cells
C) Are lined by squamous cells
D) Lie below the muscularis mucosae

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.5**
**Why A:** Ask which side of the muscularis mucosae the structure lies on.
**Why not:** B) Both do. C) Neither. D) Neither.

**Q15.** Taeniae coli represent:
A) Thickened circular muscle  B) Three bands of the outer longitudinal muscle layer  C) Mucosal folds  D) Serosal tags

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.5**
**Why B:** Their tone produces haustra.
**Why not:** A) Circular layer is complete. C) Plicae. D) Appendices epiploicae.

**Q16.** The oesophagus in the thorax is covered externally by:
A) Serosa  B) Mesothelium  C) Adventitia  D) Peritoneum

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.3**
**Why C:** Being fixed and largely retroperitoneal/mediastinal, it lacks a serosa — a frequently tested point.
**Why not:** A, B, D imply a free peritoneal surface.

**Q17.** Barrett oesophagus is diagnosed by the presence above the gastro-oesophageal junction of:
A) Squamous epithelium  B) Brunner glands  C) Parietal cells  D) Intestinal-type columnar epithelium with goblet cells

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.6**
**Why D:** Goblet cells are the decisive finding of intestinal metaplasia; it carries adenocarcinoma risk.
**Why not:** A) Normal. B, C) Not diagnostic of Barrett.

**Q18.** Helicobacter pylori survives gastric acid principally by:
A) Producing urease to generate ammonia  B) Forming spores  C) Invading parietal cells  D) Coating itself in mucin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.6**
**Why A:** Ammonia neutralises its immediate microenvironment within the mucus layer.
**Why not:** B, C) Not its biology. D) It lives in mucus but that is not the acid-resistance mechanism.

**Q19.** Gastric MALT lymphoma is notable because it:
A) Never responds to treatment
B) May regress after antibiotic eradication of H. pylori
C) Is congenital
D) Arises from parietal cells

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 12.6**
**Why B:** A lymphoma driven by chronic antigenic stimulation from the organism.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q20.** NSAIDs cause gastric injury because they:
A) Are directly caustic only
B) Stimulate gastrin
C) Inhibit cyclo-oxygenase, removing prostaglandin support of mucus, bicarbonate and mucosal blood flow
D) Kill H. pylori

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.6**
**Why C:** PGE2 maintains the mucosal defence.
**Why not:** A) A minor contribution. B, D) Incorrect.

**Q21.** Pyloric glands differ from fundic glands by having:
A) Long glands with abundant parietal cells
B) Goblet cells
C) No pits
D) Deep pits with short coiled mucous glands and few parietal cells

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.3**
**Why D:** Pit-to-gland ratio is the recognition key.
**Why not:** A) Fundus/body. B, C) Incorrect.

**Q22.** G cells secreting gastrin are located predominantly in the:
A) Antrum  B) Fundus  C) Duodenal submucosa  D) Oesophagus

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.3**
**Why A:** Gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison) causes refractory ulceration.
**Why not:** B) Parietal and chief cells. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q23.** Chief cells secrete pepsinogen, which is activated by:
A) Trypsin  B) Acid pH  C) Bile  D) Enterokinase

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 12.3**
**Why B:** Autocatalytic conversion to pepsin occurs at low pH.
**Why not:** A, D) Activate pancreatic zymogens. C) Emulsifies fat.

**Q24.** The appendix is recognised histologically by:
A) Villi  B) Brunner glands  C) A small, often irregular lumen with abundant lymphoid tissue encircling the wall  D) Parietal cells

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.5**
**Why C:** Colonic-type mucosa with prominent circumferential lymphoid aggregates.
**Why not:** A, B, D belong to other regions.

**Q25.** The gastric mucosal barrier depends on all EXCEPT:
A) An adherent mucus-bicarbonate layer  B) Tight junctions between surface cells  C) Rapid epithelial turnover  D) Absence of blood flow

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 12.6**
**Why D:** Rich mucosal blood flow is essential to remove back-diffused H⁺.
**Why not:** A, B, C are all genuine components.
