# Chapter 14 — Respiratory System

**Q1.** A bronchiole is defined by the absence of:
A) Smooth muscle  B) Cartilage and glands  C) Epithelium  D) Elastic fibres

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 14.1**
**Why B:** The bronchus-to-bronchiole transition is defined by this loss, not by diameter.
**Why not:** A) Prominent in bronchioles. C, D) Present.

**Q2.** Goblet cells disappear at the bronchiolar level because:
A) They are replaced by cilia
B) There is no mucus requirement anywhere distally
C) Mucus in cartilage-free airways would cause plugging and collapse
D) They migrate to alveoli

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.1**
**Why C:** Bronchioles are splinted only by elastic recoil of surrounding parenchyma.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect.

**Q3.** Club cells (formerly Clara cells) perform all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Secrete surfactant-associated proteins and a protease inhibitor
B) Detoxify xenobiotics via cytochrome P450
C) Act as bronchiolar progenitor cells
D) Produce mucin as their primary product

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.2**
**Why D:** They are specifically non-mucous, dome-shaped, non-ciliated cells.
**Why not:** A, B, C are all genuine functions.

**Q4.** The first structure in which gas exchange occurs is the:
A) Respiratory bronchiole  B) Terminal bronchiole  C) Alveolar duct  D) Trachea

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.1**
**Why A:** It has occasional alveoli budding from its wall; the terminal bronchiole is the last purely conducting airway.
**Why not:** B) Conducting. C) Further distal. D) Conducting.

**Q5.** The three layers of the blood-air barrier are:
A) Three cell layers
B) Type I pneumocyte, fused basal laminae, capillary endothelium
C) Type II pneumocyte, surfactant, endothelium
D) Macrophage, basal lamina, endothelium

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.3**
**Why B:** Thickness may be as little as 0.2 µm, in accordance with Fick's law.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q6.** Type II pneumocytes constitute approximately ____ of alveolar cells but cover ____ of the surface:
A) 40%; 95%  B) 5%; 60%  C) 60%; 5%  D) 95%; 40%

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.3**
**Why C:** They are more numerous but far less extensive — the classic trap.
**Why not:** A) Describes type I cells. B, D) Incorrect.

**Q7.** Lamellar bodies within type II pneumocytes contain:
A) Mucin  B) Lysosomal enzymes only  C) Glycogen  D) Surfactant

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 14.3**
**Why D:** Chiefly dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine with surfactant proteins A–D.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q8.** Surfactant stabilises small alveoli because its tension-lowering effect:
A) Is greater at smaller radius as molecules pack more densely
B) Is uniform at all radii
C) Increases surface tension
D) Only acts in large alveoli

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.4**
**Why A:** By Laplace's law (P = 2T/r), small alveoli would otherwise empty into large ones.
**Why not:** B, C, D misstate the mechanism.

**Q9.** Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome shows histologically:
A) Emphysema  B) Collapsed alveoli with eosinophilic hyaline membranes  C) Granulomas  D) Bronchiectasis

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.4**
**Why B:** Proteinaceous exudate lines the collapsed airspaces.
**Why not:** A, C, D are other pathologies.

**Q10.** Which cell is the progenitor for alveolar epithelial repair?
A) Type I pneumocyte  B) Alveolar macrophage  C) Type II pneumocyte  D) Club cell

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.3**
**Why C:** Type II hyperplasia is the histological sign of prior alveolar injury.
**Why not:** A) Terminally differentiated. B) Immune. D) Bronchiolar progenitor.

**Q11.** "Heart failure cells" are:
A) Cardiac myocytes  B) Type II pneumocytes  C) Fibroblasts  D) Haemosiderin-laden alveolar macrophages

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.5**
**Why D:** They have ingested extravasated erythrocytes; demonstrated with Prussian blue.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q12.** Centriacinar emphysema from smoking predominantly affects the:
A) Upper lobes, around respiratory bronchioles  B) Lower lobes  C) Pleura  D) Trachea

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.6**
**Why A:** Smoke is most concentrated there; α1-antitrypsin deficiency instead gives lower-lobe panacinar disease.
**Why not:** B) The α1-antitrypsin pattern. C, D) Not emphysema sites.

**Q13.** Smoking promotes emphysema partly by:
A) Increasing α1-antitrypsin
B) Recruiting neutrophils and oxidatively inactivating α1-antitrypsin
C) Reducing elastase
D) Thickening alveolar walls

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.6**
**Why B:** It shifts the protease-antiprotease balance in both directions at once.
**Why not:** A, C, D are the reverse.

**Q14.** Loss of alveolar elastin causes airflow obstruction because it:
A) Narrows the trachea
B) Increases surfactant
C) Removes elastic recoil and the radial traction splinting bronchioles, which then collapse on expiration
D) Thickens the barrier

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.6**
**Why C:** The physiology is read directly from the lost elastin.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect.

**Q15.** Curschmann spirals and Charcot-Leyden crystals are features of:
A) Emphysema  B) Pneumonia  C) Tuberculosis  D) Asthma

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.6**
**Why D:** Mucus casts of small airways and crystallised galectin-10 from eosinophils.
**Why not:** A, B, C show other findings.

**Q16.** The trachea is identified by:
A) A C-shaped cartilage with trachealis muscle bridging the posterior gap
B) Complete cartilage rings
C) Absent cartilage
D) Stratified squamous epithelium

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 14.7**
**Why A:** The open posterior gap accommodates the oesophagus.
**Why not:** B) Not complete. C) Bronchioles. D) Wrong epithelium.

**Q17.** Olfactory epithelium is distinguished from respiratory epithelium by:
A) Goblet cells
B) Greater thickness, absence of goblet cells, and large underlying nerve bundles with Bowman glands
C) Cilia
D) Stratification

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.7**
**Why B:** The combination of thickness plus large nerve fascicles beneath is decisive.
**Why not:** A) Respiratory. C) Both have cilia (non-motile in olfactory). D) Both pseudostratified.

**Q18.** Alveolar macrophages are also known as:
A) Kupffer cells  B) Langerhans cells  C) Dust cells  D) Microglia

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 14.5**
**Why C:** They phagocytose inhaled particulates and surfactant.
**Why not:** A) Liver. B) Skin. D) CNS.

**Q19.** Alveolar pores of Kohn permit:
A) Blood flow  B) Surfactant secretion  C) Lymph drainage  D) Collateral air movement between adjacent alveoli

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.3**
**Why D:** They allow equalisation of pressure and collateral ventilation — but also spread of infection.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q20.** Lung parenchyma is distinguished from adipose tissue on low power by:
A) Capillary-containing septa and the presence of bronchioles with accompanying arteries
B) Colour
C) Empty appearance
D) Cell size

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.7**
**Why A:** Adipocytes have distinct outlines with peripheral nuclei and no airways.
**Why not:** B, C) Both look pale and empty — hence the confusion. D) Unreliable.

**Q21.** Pulmonary arteries accompany bronchi and bronchioles; pulmonary veins run:
A) Alongside the airway  B) In the interlobular septa, away from airways  C) In the pleura only  D) Within alveolar walls

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.7**
**Why B:** A useful orientation rule on section.
**Why not:** A) Arteries. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q22.** The acinus of the lung comprises everything distal to a:
A) Bronchus  B) Respiratory bronchiole  C) Terminal bronchiole  D) Alveolar sac

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.1**
**Why C:** The functional respiratory unit.
**Why not:** A) Too proximal. B, D) Components within it.

**Q23.** Antenatal corticosteroids reduce neonatal RDS by:
A) Increasing lung size  B) Dilating airways  C) Reducing inflammation only  D) Accelerating type II pneumocyte maturation and surfactant production

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.4**
**Why D:** Directly targets the deficient cell.
**Why not:** A, B, C are not the mechanism.

**Q24.** The epithelium of the trachea is:
A) Pseudostratified ciliated columnar with goblet cells  B) Simple squamous  C) Stratified squamous  D) Simple cuboidal

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 14.7**
**Why A:** Adapted for mucociliary clearance, over a conspicuously thick basement membrane.
**Why not:** B) Alveoli. C) Oesophagus. D) Small bronchioles.

**Q25.** Moving distally along the airway, smooth muscle becomes ____ relative to wall thickness while cartilage ____:
A) Less prominent; increases  B) More prominent; disappears  C) Unchanged; unchanged  D) Absent; increases

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 14.1**
**Why B:** This is why bronchioles are the site of bronchoconstriction in asthma.
**Why not:** A, C, D reverse or deny the gradient.

**Q26.** Which statement about pneumocytes is correct?
A) Type II cells are the more numerous (~60% of cells) but cover only ~5% of the surface
B) Type I cells are both the more numerous and cover more surface
C) Type II cells cover ~95% of the surface
D) Type I and type II are present in equal numbers and cover equal areas

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 14.3**
**Why A:** Type I cells are ~40% of alveolar cells yet spread over ~95% of the surface because each is paper-thin; type II cells are ~60% of cells but compact and cuboidal, covering ~5%.
**Why not:** B) Type I cover more surface but are fewer. C) That is the type I figure. D) Neither number nor area is equal. Quoting only one of the two figures is what makes this a reliable trap.
