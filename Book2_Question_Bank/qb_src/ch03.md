# Chapter 3 — Nucleus

**Q1.** A large, pale, open nucleus with a prominent nucleolus indicates a cell that is:
A) Quiescent  B) Apoptotic  C) Transcriptionally and synthetically active  D) Terminally keratinised

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 3.1**
**Why C:** Euchromatin is decondensed and accessible; a large nucleolus means active ribosome production.
**Why not:** A) Would be small and dense. B) Pyknotic/fragmented. D) Anucleate.

**Q2.** Heterochromatin appears dark in H&E because it is:
A) Rich in protein only  B) Calcified  C) Bound to eosin  D) Densely packed, concentrating DNA phosphate per unit volume

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.1**
**Why D:** Compaction raises local anionic density, so more haematoxylin binds per unit area.
**Why not:** A) DNA is the key binder. C) It is basophilic. B) Not mineralised.

**Q3.** The number of Barr bodies in a 47,XXY (Klinefelter) cell is:
A) 1  B) 0  C) 2  D) 3

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.2**
**Why A:** Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes minus one.
**Why not:** B) 46,XY or 45,X. C) 47,XXX. D) 48,XXXX.

**Q4.** X-inactivation in females produces mosaicism because it is:
A) Uniform across all cells
B) Random in each cell but clonally inherited thereafter
C) Determined by paternal origin always
D) Reversed at puberty

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.2**
**Why B:** Random early choice, faithfully propagated, creates patches of two cell populations.
**Why not:** A) Would preclude mosaicism. C) Random, not imprinted, in humans generally. D) It is stable.

**Q5.** The current evidence regarding the 30-nm chromatin fibre is best stated as:
A) It is the proven universal structure of interphase chromatin
B) It does not exist under any condition
C) It forms in vitro and in some specialised chromatins, but is not demonstrated as a regular structure in typical interphase nuclei
D) It is composed of microtubules

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.1**
**Why C:** Cryo-EM, SAXS and Hi-B favour irregularly folded, interdigitated 10-nm fibres in a disordered state in vivo.
**Why not:** A) Overstates the evidence. B) Understates it — it is demonstrable in vitro and in starfish sperm/avian erythrocytes. D) Wrong polymer entirely.

**Q6.** Nuclear import of a protein bearing an NLS requires:
A) Exportin and Ran-GDP  B) Vesicular transport  C) Passive diffusion only  D) Importin, with cargo release by Ran-GTP in the nucleus

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.3**
**Why D:** Ran-GTP is high in the nucleus (Ran-GEF), causing importin to release cargo there.
**Why not:** A) Describes export. C) Only for molecules under ~40 kDa. B) The envelope is not crossed by vesicles.

**Q7.** The nucleolus is basophilic principally because it contains abundant:
A) rRNA  B) DNA only  C) Lipid  D) Calcium

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 3.4**
**Why A:** rRNB transcription and ribosome assembly concentrate RNB there.
**Why not:** B) DNB contributes, but rRNB dominates. C, D) Not nucleolar constituents.

**Q8.** Nucleolar organiser regions are located on the:
A) Metacentric chromosomes  B) Acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, 22  C) Sex chromosomes  D) Chromosome 1 only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.4**
**Why B:** Their short arms carry the tandem rRNA gene repeats.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect assignments.

**Q9.** Apoptosis is distinguished from necrosis by:
A) Cell swelling and inflammation
B) Karyolysis
C) Cell shrinkage, membrane integrity, and absence of inflammation
D) Neutrophil infiltration

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 3.5**
**Why C:** Apoptosis is an ordered, ATP-dependent, caspase-mediated, non-inflammatory process.
**Why not:** A, B, D are all features of necrosis.

**Q10.** The intrinsic (mitochondrial) apoptotic pathway is initiated by:
A) Fas ligand binding Fas
B) Perforin insertion
C) Caspase-8 activation at the membrane
D) Cytochrome c release forming the apoptosome and activating caspase-9

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.5**
**Why D:** Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilisation releases cytochrome c, which with Apaf-1 forms the apoptosome.
**Why not:** A, C) Extrinsic death-receptor pathway. B) Cytotoxic T-cell mechanism.

**Q11.** Coagulative necrosis is characteristic of:
A) Most solid organ ischaemia  B) Brain infarction  C) Tuberculosis  D) Acute pancreatitis

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.5**
**Why A:** Protein denaturation preserves tissue outlines while nuclei are lost.
**Why not:** B) Liquefactive. C) Caseous. D) Fat necrosis.

**Q12.** Liquefactive necrosis is typical of the brain because:
A) The brain has abundant collagen
B) High lipid content and abundant hydrolytic enzymes with little supporting stroma
C) It is avascular
D) It calcifies rapidly

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.5**
**Why B:** Enzymatic digestion dominates and there is no collagenous framework to preserve outlines.
**Why not:** A) The opposite. C) It is highly vascular. D) Not a feature.

**Q13.** Pyknosis refers to:
A) Nuclear fragmentation  B) Nuclear dissolution  C) Nuclear shrinkage and chromatin condensation  D) Nucleolar enlargement

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 3.5**
**Why C:** Pyknosis → karyorrhexis (fragmentation) → karyolysis (dissolution) is the sequence.
**Why not:** A) Karyorrhexis. B) Karyolysis. D) Unrelated.

**Q14.** The restriction point of the cell cycle occurs in:
A) Early G1  B) G2  C) S phase  D) Late G1

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.6**
**Why D:** After it, the cell is committed to divide irrespective of extracellular signals.
**Why not:** A) Still signal-dependent. B, C) Already committed.

**Q15.** Rb protein normally restrains the cycle by:
A) Sequestering E2F transcription factors until phosphorylated  B) Degrading cyclins  C) Activating CDK1  D) Inducing apoptosis

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.6**
**Why A:** Cyclin D-CDK4/6 phosphorylates Rb, releasing E2F to drive S-phase genes.
**Why not:** B) Not its mechanism. C) Rb inhibits progression. D) That is p53's role.

**Q16.** Cyclin B-CDK1 drives the transition:
A) G1/S  B) G2/M  C) S/G2  D) M/G1

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.6**
**Why B:** Classically maturation-promoting factor.
**Why not:** A) Cyclin E-CDK2. C) Cyclin A. D) Governed by cyclin destruction.

**Q17.** Autophagy is best characterised as:
A) Uncontrolled cell rupture
B) Caspase-driven fragmentation
C) Regulated self-digestion via autophagosome–lysosome fusion
D) Phagocytosis of neighbouring cells

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.5**
**Why C:** Phagophore → autophagosome → autolysosome, with LC3 as the marker; provides substrates and quality control.
**Why not:** A) Necrosis. B) Apoptosis. D) Heterophagy.

**Q18.** Nuclear lamins are:
A) Microtubules  B) Integral membrane pumps  C) Actin filaments  D) Intermediate filaments lining the inner nuclear membrane

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.1**
**Why D:** Lamins A, D, C form the lamina, supporting the envelope and tethering heterochromatin.
**Why not:** A, C) Cytoplasmic systems. B) Not transporters.

**Q19.** Laminopathies such as Hutchinson-Gilford progeria arise from defects in:
A) LMNA  B) COL1A1  C) FBN1  D) DMD

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.1**
**Why A:** Abnormal lamin A (progerin) distorts the nuclear envelope.
**Why not:** B) Osteogenesis imperfecta. C) Marfan. D) Duchenne muscular dystrophy.

**Q20.** A "starry sky" pattern in a lymphoid proliferation is produced by:
A) Necrotic debris  B) Tingible-body macrophages engulfing apoptotic cells  C) Fibrosis  D) Amyloid

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.5**
**Why B:** Pale macrophages against a dark background of proliferating cells, reflecting high apoptotic turnover.
**Why not:** A) Necrosis produces confluent, not scattered pale cells. C, D) Different appearances.

**Q21.** In malignancy, the increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio reflects:
A) Cytoplasmic hypertrophy
B) Nuclear shrinkage
C) Nuclear enlargement with relatively scant cytoplasm in rapidly dividing, poorly differentiated cells
D) Loss of the nucleolus

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.7**
**Why C:** Proliferating undifferentiated cells invest in nucleus over specialised cytoplasm.
**Why not:** A, B) Opposite. D) Nucleoli are typically prominent.

**Q22.** Prominent nucleoli alone are NOT diagnostic of malignancy because they also occur in:
A) Apoptotic cells  B) Erythrocytes  C) Keratinocytes of the corneum  D) Plasma cells, neurons, hepatocytes and regenerating tissue

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.7**
**Why D:** Nucleolar prominence means ribosome production, common to any synthetically active cell.
**Why not:** A) Nucleoli are lost. B, C) Anucleate or nearly so.

**Q23.** Facultative heterochromatin differs from constitutive heterochromatin in that it:
A) Can be reactivated in other cell types  B) Is never transcribed  C) Is found only at centromeres  D) Lacks histones

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.1**
**Why A:** It represents tissue-specific silencing of genes that are active elsewhere.
**Why not:** B, C) Describe constitutive heterochromatin. D) All chromatin has histones.

**Q24.** Nuclear pore complexes permit free diffusion of molecules up to approximately:
A) 4 kDa  B) 40 kDa  C) 400 kDa  D) No passive diffusion occurs

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.3**
**Why B:** Larger cargo requires signal-mediated, Ran-dependent transport.
**Why not:** A) Too restrictive. C) Too permissive. D) Small molecules do diffuse.

**Q25.** Distinguishing apoptosis from necrosis on a section is best achieved by noting that apoptosis affects:
A) Confluent sheets of cells with neutrophils
B) Only the nucleus
C) Scattered single cells with intact membranes and no inflammatory infiltrate
D) Only epithelium

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.5**
**Why C:** Distribution is the decisive feature: single-cell death in an otherwise healthy field.
**Why not:** A) Describes necrosis. B, D) Neither is true.
