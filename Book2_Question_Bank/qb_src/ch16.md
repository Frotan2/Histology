# Chapter 16 — Endocrine System

**Q1.** Tubular mitochondrial cristae and abundant smooth ER indicate a cell secreting:
A) Peptide hormone  B) Mucus  C) Catecholamine  D) Steroid hormone

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.1**
**Why D:** Steroidogenic P450 enzymes reside in SER and tubular-cristae mitochondria.
**Why not:** A) RER, Golgi, granules. C) Dense-core granules. B) Exocrine.

**Q2.** Steroid hormones are not stored in secretory granules because they:
A) Are lipid-soluble and diffuse through membranes  B) Are too large  C) Are toxic  D) Degrade rapidly

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.1**
**Why A:** They must therefore be synthesised on demand, making steroid responses slower than peptide responses.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q3.** The anterior pituitary develops from:
A) Neuroectoderm  B) Oral ectoderm (Rathke's pouch)  C) Mesoderm  D) Endoderm

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.2**
**Why B:** A true glandular epithelium, unlike the neural posterior lobe.
**Why not:** A) Posterior pituitary. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q4.** The posterior pituitary:
A) Synthesises ADH and oxytocin
B) Contains acidophils and basophils
C) Stores and releases hormones made in the hypothalamus
D) Is controlled by a portal system

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 16.2**
**Why C:** Made in supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei and transported down axons.
**Why not:** A) It synthesises none. B) Anterior lobe. D) Neural, not vascular, control.

**Q5.** Herring bodies are:
A) Colloid droplets  B) Secretory granules of acidophils  C) Glial nuclei  D) Dilated axon terminals with neurosecretory granules

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.2**
**Why D:** A decisive recognition feature of the neurohypophysis, along with pituicytes.
**Why not:** A) Thyroid. C) Pituicytes are the glia themselves. B) Anterior lobe.

**Q6.** Basophils of the anterior pituitary produce (mnemonic B-FLAT):
A) FSH, LH, ACTH, TSH  B) GH and prolactin  C) ADH and oxytocin  D) Calcitonin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.2**
**Why A:** Acidophils make GH and prolactin.
**Why not:** B) Acidophils. C) Posterior lobe. D) Thyroid C cells.

**Q7.** The hypophyseal portal system is so called because it has:
A) One capillary bed  B) Two capillary beds in series  C) No veins  D) Arterial anastomoses only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.2**
**Why B:** It delivers hypothalamic hormones at high concentration without systemic dilution.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q8.** Prolactin is unique among anterior pituitary hormones in being under predominantly:
A) Stimulatory control  B) No control  C) Inhibitory control by dopamine  D) Direct neural control

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.2**
**Why C:** Stalk section therefore *raises* prolactin while lowering all other anterior pituitary hormones.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect.

**Q9.** The thyroid is unique among endocrine glands in that it:
A) Has no blood supply
B) Lacks a capsule
C) Secretes only steroids
D) Stores its hormone extracellularly as colloid

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.3**
**Why D:** Thyroglobulin in the follicular lumen holds months of supply.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q10.** Thyroid peroxidase catalyses:
A) Oxidation of iodide and organification of tyrosine residues  B) Iodide trapping  C) Colloid endocytosis  D) T4-to-T3 conversion in the periphery

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.3**
**Why A:** It is the target of thionamide antithyroid drugs and the antigen in Hashimoto thyroiditis.
**Why not:** B) NIS. C) Separate step. D) Deiodinases.

**Q11.** The sodium-iodide symporter (NIS) is inhibited by:
A) Carbimazole  B) Perchlorate  C) Propranolol  D) Levothyroxine

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.3**
**Why B:** It competes for the transporter; radioiodine uptake also exploits NIS.
**Why not:** A) Inhibits TPO. C) Symptomatic. D) Replacement.

**Q12.** Scalloped resorption vacuoles at the colloid margin with tall columnar follicular cells indicate:
A) An inactive gland  B) Neoplasia  C) An actively stimulated gland  D) Fibrosis

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.3**
**Why C:** A direct visual diagnosis of hyperstimulation, as in Graves disease.
**Why not:** A) Flat cells, dense distended colloid. B, D) Different features.

**Q13.** Current lineage-tracing evidence indicates thyroid C cells derive from:
A) Neural crest  B) Surface ectoderm  C) Mesoderm  D) Pharyngeal endoderm via the ultimobranchial body

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.3**
**Why D:** Sox17-Cre lineage tracing shows an endodermal origin, overturning the older neural crest teaching (which many examinations still use).
**Why not:** A) The classical but superseded answer. B, C) Incorrect.

**Q14.** Medullary thyroid carcinoma arises from C cells and characteristically shows:
A) Amyloid stroma derived from calcitonin  B) Psammoma bodies  C) Colloid-filled follicles  D) Hürthle cells

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.3**
**Why A:** It occurs in MEN 2B and 2A and secretes calcitonin.
**Why not:** B) Papillary carcinoma. C) Normal/follicular. D) Hashimoto/Hürthle cell tumour.

**Q15.** PTH stimulates osteoclasts:
A) Directly via osteoclast PTH receptors
B) Indirectly, by acting on osteoblasts to increase RANKL
C) Through calcitonin
D) Via vitamin D only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.4**
**Why B:** Osteoclasts lack PTH receptors — a classic trap.
**Why not:** A) False. C) Opposes PTH. D) One of several PTH effects.

**Q16.** Primary hyperparathyroidism produces:
A) High calcium, high phosphate  B) Low calcium, low phosphate  C) High calcium, low phosphate  D) Low calcium, high phosphate

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.4**
**Why C:** PTH raises calcium while promoting renal phosphate excretion.
**Why not:** A, B, D are inconsistent with PTH action.

**Q17.** Oxyphil cells of the parathyroid are:
A) The source of PTH
B) Chief cell precursors only
C) Adipocytes
D) Large eosinophilic mitochondria-rich cells of uncertain function, increasing after puberty

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.4**
**Why D:** Chief cells produce PTH.
**Why not:** A) Chief cells. B, C) Incorrect.

**Q18.** The zona fasciculata secretes ____ and appears ____:
A) Cortisol; pale and vacuolated (spongiocytes)  B) Aldosterone; dark  C) Androgens; eosinophilic  D) Catecholamines; basophilic

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 16.5**
**Why A:** It holds the most lipid (cholesterol ester precursor), extracted in processing.
**Why not:** B) Glomerulosa. C) Reticularis. D) Medulla.

**Q19.** Aldosterone secretion by the zona glomerulosa is regulated chiefly by:
A) ACTH  B) Renin-angiotensin and potassium  C) TSH  D) Dopamine

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.5**
**Why B:** This is why glomerulosa function is relatively preserved in isolated ACTH deficiency.
**Why not:** A) Fasciculata and reticularis. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q20.** The adrenal medulla produces mostly adrenaline (rather than noradrenaline) because:
A) It lacks noradrenaline
B) It is innervated postganglionically
C) Cortisol arriving from the cortex induces PNMT
D) It is neural crest derived

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.5**
**Why C:** The corticomedullary blood flow gradient explains why extra-adrenal chromaffin tissue makes noradrenaline instead.
**Why not:** A) It makes both. B) Innervated *pre*ganglionically. D) True but not the reason.

**Q21.** Adrenal medullary chromaffin cells are innervated by:
A) Postganglionic sympathetic fibres
B) Somatic motor fibres
C) Parasympathetic fibres
D) Preganglionic sympathetic fibres

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.5**
**Why D:** They are modified postganglionic neurons that have lost their axons, so they respond instantly.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q22.** In 21-hydroxylase deficiency, adrenal hyperplasia occurs because:
A) Cortisol deficiency removes negative feedback, raising ACTH
B) ACTH is suppressed
C) Aldosterone is excessive
D) The adrenal is infiltrated

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.5**
**Why A:** Precursors are shunted to androgens, causing virilisation, with salt wasting if aldosterone is also deficient.
**Why not:** B) ACTH rises. C) It is deficient. D) Not the mechanism.

**Q23.** Beta cells constitute about ____ of islet cells and are located ____:
A) 20%; peripherally  B) 70%; centrally  C) 5%; scattered  D) 50%; peripherally

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.6**
**Why B:** Alpha cells (~20%) are peripheral; delta cells are scattered.
**Why not:** A) Alpha. C) Delta. D) Incorrect.

**Q24.** Islet amyloid in type 2 diabetes is derived from:
A) Insulin  B) Calcitonin  C) Islet amyloid polypeptide (amylin), co-secreted with insulin  D) Immunoglobulin light chains

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 16.6**
**Why C:** Type 1 diabetes instead shows insulitis with beta-cell depletion.
**Why not:** A) Not directly. B) Medullary thyroid carcinoma. D) AL amyloidosis.

**Q25.** Adrenal cortex may be confused with liver on low power; the decisive discriminator is:
A) Cell colour
B) Nuclear shape
C) Cell size
D) A thick capsule with zonation and lipid-rich vacuolated cells versus portal triads and binucleate hepatocytes

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 16.7**
**Why D:** Find a portal triad (liver) or a zoned cortex beneath a capsule (adrenal).
**Why not:** A) Both form eosinophilic cords — hence the confusion. B, C) Unreliable.
