# Chapter 13 — Organs Associated with the Digestive System

**Q1.** In the classic hepatic lobule, blood flows:
A) From the portal triads to the central vein, while bile flows the opposite way
B) From the central vein to the portal triads
C) In the same direction as bile
D) Only through lymphatics

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 13.1**
**Why A:** Blood and bile flow in opposite directions — the key orientation fact.
**Why not:** B) Reversed. C) They are counter-current. D) Incorrect.

**Q2.** The portal triad contains:
A) Central vein, artery, lymphatic
B) Portal venule, hepatic arteriole, bile ductule
C) Two veins and a nerve
D) Sinusoid and canaliculus

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 13.1**
**Why B:** Plus lymphatics and nerves — the decisive feature identifying liver.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q3.** Centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis following shock occurs because zone 3 is:
A) Nearest the portal triad  B) Richest in glycogen  C) Farthest along the oxygen gradient  D) Avascular

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.2**
**Why C:** Lowest oxygen tension makes it most vulnerable to hypoxia — the "nutmeg liver."
**Why not:** A) That is zone 1. B, D) Incorrect.

**Q4.** Paracetamol toxicity also damages zone 3, but because that zone:
A) Is hypoxic  B) Is nearest the portal vein  C) Lacks glutathione normally  D) Has the highest cytochrome P450 activity, generating NAPQI

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.2**
**Why D:** Two mechanisms, same zone, different logic — a favourite examination discrimination.
**Why not:** A) The mechanism for ischaemic injury. C) Glutathione is depleted, not absent at baseline. B) Incorrect.

**Q5.** Hepatic stellate (Ito) cells normally store:
A) Vitamin A  B) Iron  C) Glycogen  D) Copper

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.3**
**Why A:** They hold about 80% of the body's vitamin B in lipid droplets, located in the space of Disse.
**Why not:** B) Kupffer cells/hepatocytes. C) Hepatocytes. D) Not a storage cell for copper.

**Q6.** The central cell of hepatic fibrosis is the:
A) Kupffer cell  B) Activated stellate cell transdifferentiating into a myofibroblast  C) Hepatocyte  D) Pit cell

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.3**
**Why B:** It loses lipid droplets and secretes collagen into the space of Disse.
**Why not:** A) Macrophage. C) Parenchymal. D) NK cell.

**Q7.** Kupffer cells are located:
A) In the space of Disse  B) In portal tracts only  C) Within the sinusoidal lumen  D) Inside hepatocytes

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.3**
**Why C:** Their intraluminal position distinguishes them from stellate cells and lets them clear portal-borne bacteria.
**Why not:** A) Stellate cells. B, D) Incorrect.

**Q8.** The space of Disse is significant because:
A) It contains bile
B) It stores glycogen
C) It is a lymphatic vessel
D) Plasma contacts hepatocyte microvilli directly across a discontinuous endothelium with deficient basal lamina

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.3**
**Why D:** This unrestricted access underlies the liver's synthetic and detoxifying role; hepatic lymph forms here.
**Why not:** A) Canaliculi. C) Lymph forms here but it is not a vessel. B) Intracellular.

**Q9.** The bile canaliculus is:
A) A groove between apposed apical membranes of two hepatocytes, sealed by tight junctions
B) A duct lined by cholangiocytes
C) A sinusoid
D) A lymphatic

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.4**
**Why A:** The hepatocyte is polarised, facing blood on one side and bile on the other.
**Why not:** B) Ductules, further downstream. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q10.** Obstructive (post-hepatic) jaundice produces:
A) Unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
B) Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia with pale stools and dark urine
C) Normal bilirubin
D) Only raised transaminases

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.4**
**Why B:** Water-soluble conjugated bilirubin is excreted renally, darkening urine.
**Why not:** A) Haemolysis or conjugation defects. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q11.** Gilbert syndrome affects which step of bilirubin handling?
A) Uptake  B) Canalicular excretion  C) Conjugation (reduced UGT1A1 activity)  D) Enterohepatic recycling

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.4**
**Why C:** Mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, often manifest during fasting or illness.
**Why not:** A) Minor. B) Dubin-Johnson. D) Not the defect.

**Q12.** Dubin-Johnson syndrome produces a grossly black liver because of:
A) Iron  B) Copper  C) Melanin  D) Defective MRP2 canalicular excretion with pigment accumulation

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.4**
**Why D:** Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia from an excretion defect.
**Why not:** A) Haemochromatosis. C) Not hepatic. B) Wilson disease.

**Q13.** Cirrhosis causes ascites and varices chiefly through:
A) Architectural distortion obstructing portal flow, raising portal pressure
B) Reduced albumin only
C) Kupffer cell loss
D) Bile duct proliferation

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.5**
**Why A:** The mechanical consequence; the synthetic consequence explains coagulopathy and hypoalbuminaemia.
**Why not:** B) Contributory but not primary for varices. C, D) Not the mechanism.

**Q14.** Centroacinar cells are unique to the:
A) Parotid  B) Pancreas  C) Liver  D) Gallbladder

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.6**
**Why B:** Intercalated duct cells extending into the acinar lumen — a decisive identifying feature.
**Why not:** A, C, D do not possess them.

**Q15.** Pancreatic bicarbonate secretion is performed by:
A) Acinar cells  B) Islet cells  C) Duct and centroacinar cells via CFTR  D) Stellate cells

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.6**
**Why C:** Acinar cells make enzymes; ducts make the alkaline fluid those enzymes require.
**Why not:** A) Enzymes. B) Hormones. D) Fibrosis.

**Q16.** In cystic fibrosis, exocrine pancreatic insufficiency results from:
A) Autoimmune destruction
B) Islet destruction
C) Absent acinar cells from birth
D) Viscid secretions obstructing ducts, causing autodigestion, atrophy and fibrosis

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.6**
**Why D:** Islets are relatively spared until late.
**Why not:** A) Type 1 diabetes mechanism. B, C) Incorrect.

**Q17.** Acute pancreatitis results fundamentally from:
A) Premature intrapancreatic activation of trypsinogen initiating the enzyme cascade
B) Bacterial infection
C) Insulin deficiency
D) Bile reflux only

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.7**
**Why A:** The zymogen safety system (enterokinase in duodenum; SPINK1 inhibitor) is bypassed.
**Why not:** B) Usually secondary. C) Unrelated. D) One possible trigger, not the mechanism.

**Q18.** Hypocalcaemia in acute pancreatitis results from:
A) Parathyroid failure
B) Calcium chelation by fatty acids released by lipase (saponification)
C) Vitamin D deficiency
D) Renal loss

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.7**
**Why B:** Fat necrosis produces chalky calcium soaps — a clinical sign traceable to histology.
**Why not:** A, C, D are not the mechanism.

**Q19.** The pancreas is distinguished from the parotid gland by the presence of:
A) Striated ducts  B) Adipose tissue  C) Islets of Langerhans and centroacinar cells  D) Serous acini

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.8**
**Why C:** The parotid has striated ducts and no islets; the pancreas has islets and no striated ducts.
**Why not:** A) Salivary. B) Salivary lobules. D) Both are serous — hence the confusion.

**Q20.** Striated ducts appear striated because of:
A) Collagen fibres  B) Myofibrils  C) Cilia  D) Basal membrane infoldings with interposed mitochondria

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.9**
**Why D:** The arrangement supports active ion transport, modifying saliva.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q21.** The gallbladder wall is unique among hollow digestive organs in lacking:
A) A muscularis mucosae and submucosa  B) Epithelium  C) Smooth muscle  D) Serosa entirely

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.10**
**Why A:** The decisive recognition feature, often with Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses.
**Why not:** B, C) Both present. D) Partially covered by serosa.

**Q22.** The parotid gland is predominantly:
A) Mucous  B) Serous  C) Mixed mucous-predominant  D) Sebaceous

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 13.9**
**Why B:** Submandibular is mixed serous-predominant; sublingual is mucous-predominant.
**Why not:** A, C) Other glands. D) Skin.

**Q23.** Sialolithiasis most commonly affects the submandibular gland because:
A) It is the largest
B) It is serous
C) Its secretion is more viscous and its duct has an uphill course
D) It lacks myoepithelial cells

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 13.9**
**Why C:** Both factors promote stasis and stone formation.
**Why not:** A) The parotid is larger. B) It is mixed. D) They are present.

**Q24.** Myoepithelial cells in salivary glands function to:
A) Secrete enzymes  B) Produce mucin  C) Absorb water  D) Contract and expel secretion from acini

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 13.9**
**Why D:** They lie between the acinar cells and the basal lamina.
**Why not:** A, B) Acinar cells. C) Ducts.

**Q25.** The hepatic acinus of Rappaport is preferred to the classic lobule when the purpose is to:
A) Predict the zonal distribution of injury  B) Describe bile drainage  C) Identify the organ  D) Count hepatocytes

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 13.2**
**Why A:** It is the functional unit defined by proximity to the arriving blood supply.
**Why not:** B) Portal lobule. C) Classic lobule. D) Not its purpose.
