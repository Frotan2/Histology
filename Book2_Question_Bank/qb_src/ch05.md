# Chapter 5 — Connective Tissue

**Q1.** Vitamin C is required for collagen synthesis as a cofactor for:
A) Prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases  B) Lysyl oxidase  C) Procollagen peptidase  D) Galactosyltransferase

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.2**
**Why A:** Ascorbate keeps the enzyme iron reduced; hydroxyproline is needed to stabilise the triple helix.
**Why not:** B) Copper-dependent, acts at cross-linking. C) Cleaves propeptides extracellularly. D) Not vitamin C dependent.

**Q2.** Lysyl oxidase requires which trace element?
A) Iron  B) Copper  C) Zinc  D) Selenium

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.2**
**Why B:** Copper deficiency (Menkes) and lathyrism both impair cross-linking, causing connective tissue and vascular fragility.
**Why not:** A) Needed by hydroxylases. C, D) Not the cofactor here.

**Q3.** Scurvy produces bleeding gums and poor wound healing because:
A) Platelets are deficient  B) Elastin is absent  C) Collagen triple helix is unstable without hydroxyproline  D) Fibrin cannot form

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.2**
**Why C:** Under-hydroxylated collagen is degraded, weakening vessel walls and new matrix.
**Why not:** A, D) Coagulation is normal. B) Elastin is not the primary lesion.

**Q4.** Vascular (type IV) Ehlers-Danlos syndrome involves defective:
A) Type I collagen  B) Fibrillin  C) Type II collagen  D) Type III collagen

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 5.2**
**Why D:** Type III is prominent in vessel walls and bowel; its defect causes arterial and intestinal rupture — the lethal subtype.
**Why not:** A) Osteogenesis imperfecta. C) Cartilage disorders. B) Marfan.

**Q5.** Osteogenesis imperfecta most commonly results from mutations in:
A) COL1A1/COL1A2  B) COL3A1  C) FBN1  D) COL4A5

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.2**
**Why A:** Defective type I collagen causes fragile bones, blue sclerae, and hearing loss.
**Why not:** B) Vascular EDS. C) Marfan. D) Alport.

**Q6.** Reticular fibres are argyrophilic and PAS-positive because they are:
A) Type I collagen in thick bundles
B) Type III collagen with an abundant glycoprotein/proteoglycan coat
C) Composed of elastin
D) Mineralised

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.3**
**Why B:** The carbohydrate coat binds silver and reacts with PAS.
**Why not:** A) Type I is not argyrophilic in this way. C) Elastic stains differ. D) Not mineralised.

**Q7.** Reticular fibres form the supporting stroma of:
A) Tendon  B) Aorta  C) Lymph node, spleen, bone marrow and liver  D) Cornea

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.3**
**Why C:** A delicate meshwork supports soft cellular parenchyma while permitting cell and fluid movement.
**Why not:** A, D) Dense type I collagen. B) Elastic laminae.

**Q8.** Elastic fibres consist of:
A) Keratin
B) Type I collagen only
C) Proteoglycan aggregates
D) Elastin core on a fibrillin-1 microfibril scaffold

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.3**
**Why D:** Elastin is cross-linked by desmosine/isodesmosine and deposited on fibrillin microfibrils.
**Why not:** A, B, C are different matrix components.

**Q9.** In Marfan syndrome, aortic pathology results from FBN1 mutation causing:
A) Defective microfibril scaffold plus excess TGF-β signalling
B) Excess collagen deposition
C) Elastase deficiency
D) Calcification of the media

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 5.3**
**Why A:** Fibrillin-1 normally sequesters latent TGF-β; loss weakens the media mechanically and dysregulates signalling.
**Why not:** B, C, D are not the Marfan mechanism.

**Q10.** Glycosaminoglycans resist compression because they:
A) Are stiff protein rods
B) Are polyanionic, attracting cations and osmotically bound water
C) Cross-link collagen
D) Form fibrils

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.4**
**Why B:** The hydrated gel creates swelling pressure balanced by the collagen network.
**Why not:** A, D) They are not fibrillar. C) That is lysyl oxidase's role.

**Q11.** Hyaluronan differs from other glycosaminoglycans in that it is:
A) Sulfated and protein-linked
B) Found only in bone
C) Unsulfated, very large, and not covalently attached to a core protein
D) A protein

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 5.4**
**Why C:** It forms the backbone to which aggrecan monomers attach via link proteins.
**Why not:** A) True of the others. B) Widely distributed. D) It is a carbohydrate.

**Q12.** Hurler syndrome is caused by deficiency of:
A) Glucocerebrosidase  B) Iduronate sulfatase  C) Hexosaminidase A  D) α-L-iduronidase

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 5.4**
**Why D:** Autosomal recessive MPS I with corneal clouding; Hunter (iduronate sulfatase) is X-linked without corneal clouding.
**Why not:** B) Hunter. C) Tay-Sachs. A) Gaucher.

**Q13.** Mast cell granules are metachromatic because they contain:
A) Densely sulfated heparin  B) Histamine only  C) Melanin  D) Lipid

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.5**
**Why A:** Densely packed polyanions shift toluidine blue from blue to purple.
**Why not:** B) Histamine is not the metachromatic agent. C, D) Not present.

**Q14.** Mast cell degranulation in immediate hypersensitivity is triggered by:
A) Complement only  B) Cross-linking of IgE bound to FcεRI by antigen  C) IgG binding  D) Direct T-cell contact

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.5**
**Why B:** Bridging adjacent IgE molecules initiates the signalling that releases preformed and newly synthesised mediators.
**Why not:** A, C, D are not the classical type I mechanism.

**Q15.** The plasma cell is recognised by:
A) Kidney-shaped nucleus  B) Bilobed nucleus with red granules  C) Eccentric clock-face nucleus with a perinuclear hof  D) Multilobed nucleus

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.5**
**Why C:** Peripheral heterochromatin clumping plus an unstained Golgi.
**Why not:** A) Monocyte. B) Eosinophil. D) Neutrophil.

**Q16.** Dense regular connective tissue is distinguished from smooth muscle on H&E by:
A) Colour of the cytoplasm
B) Cell size
C) Presence of capillaries
D) Nuclei lying *between* acellular pink bundles rather than within eosinophilic cells

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.6**
**Why D:** In tendon, flattened fibroblast nuclei sit in rows between collagen; in muscle, cigar-shaped nuclei lie inside the cells.
**Why not:** A) Both are pink on H&E — hence the confusion; trichrome separates them. B, C) Unreliable.

**Q17.** Masson trichrome stains collagen ____ and muscle ____:
A) Blue/green; red  B) Red; blue  C) Black; yellow  D) Yellow; blue

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.3**
**Why A:** This is the basis for assessing fibrosis.
**Why not:** B) Reversed. C, D) Wrong stain conventions.

**Q18.** Dense irregular connective tissue is found in the dermis because it must resist:
A) Tension in one direction only  B) Stress from multiple directions  C) Compression only  D) No mechanical load

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.6**
**Why B:** Interwoven bundles in many orientations provide multidirectional strength.
**Why not:** A) Dense regular (tendon). C) Cartilage. D) False.

**Q19.** The fibroblast is distinguished from the fibrocyte by having:
A) A small, dark, condensed nucleus
B) Phagocytic vacuoles
C) A large, pale, euchromatic nucleus with basophilic cytoplasm
D) Metachromatic granules

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.5**
**Why C:** The active synthetic form has open chromatin and abundant RER.
**Why not:** A) The quiescent fibrocyte. B) Macrophage. D) Mast cell.

**Q20.** Macrophages in connective tissue derive from:
A) Fibroblasts  B) Endothelium  C) Mast cells  D) Blood monocytes

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.5**
**Why D:** Circulating monocytes enter tissue and differentiate (with an additional embryonic-derived resident population in some organs).
**Why not:** A, B, C are separate lineages.

**Q21.** Type II collagen is the principal fibrillar collagen of:
A) Hyaline cartilage  B) Bone  C) Basement membrane  D) Skin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.2**
**Why A:** Also present in the vitreous and intervertebral disc nucleus pulposus.
**Why not:** B, D) Type I. C) Type IV.

**Q22.** The 67 nm banding of collagen fibrils on EM results from:
A) Mineral deposits  B) Quarter-stagger overlap of tropocollagen molecules  C) Elastin cross-links  D) Proteoglycan spacing

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 5.2**
**Why B:** Regular offsetting creates alternating overlap and gap zones that bind stain differently.
**Why not:** A, C, D do not generate this periodicity.

**Q23.** Propeptide cleavage of procollagen occurs:
A) In the RER  B) In the Golgi  C) Extracellularly  D) In the nucleus

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 5.2**
**Why C:** Keeping assembly extracellular prevents the cell from being destroyed by intracellular fibril formation.
**Why not:** A, B) Would cause intracellular assembly. D) Not a site of protein processing.

**Q24.** Loose (areolar) connective tissue is characterised by:
A) Few cells and thick parallel bundles
B) Exclusively adipocytes
C) Absence of ground substance
D) Many cell types, sparse randomly arranged fibres, abundant ground substance and capillaries

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 5.6**
**Why D:** It lies beneath epithelia and is the main site of the inflammatory response.
**Why not:** A) Dense regular. B, C) Incorrect.

**Q25.** A patient with hyperextensible skin, hypermobile joints, and poor wound healing, whose defect lies in removal of procollagen N-propeptides, has:
A) A form of Ehlers-Danlos syndrome  B) Marfan syndrome  C) Scurvy  D) Osteogenesis imperfecta

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 5.2**
**Why A:** Defective propeptide cleavage impairs normal fibril assembly — a recognised EDS mechanism.
**Why not:** B) FBN1. C) Acquired vitamin deficiency with different features. D) Bone fragility predominates.
