# Chapter 8 — Circulatory System

**Q1.** Elastic arteries store energy in systole and recoil in diastole because their media contains:
A) Mostly smooth muscle  B) Cartilage  C) Only collagen  D) Concentric fenestrated elastic lamellae

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 8.1**
**Why D:** The Windkessel effect converts pulsatile ejection into more continuous flow.
**Why not:** A) Muscular arteries. B, C) Not media components of large arteries.

**Q2.** The chief site of peripheral vascular resistance is the:
A) Arteriole  B) Muscular artery  C) Aorta  D) Vein

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 8.1**
**Why A:** Small radius and a 1–3 layer muscular media make arterioles the main regulators of blood pressure.
**Why not:** B, C) Conducting/distributing. D) Capacitance vessels.

**Q3.** On H&E, an artery is distinguished from its accompanying vein by:
A) Larger lumen  B) A thick round muscular wall with a wavy internal elastic lamina  C) Thicker adventitia  D) Presence of valves

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 8.5**
**Why B:** The wavy IEL is the decisive feature; the vein collapses with a thin wall.
**Why not:** A, C, D are all venous characteristics.

**Q4.** Leukocyte diapedesis occurs predominantly at:
A) Arterioles  B) Capillaries  C) Postcapillary venules  D) Large veins

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.2**
**Why C:** Their thin walls and adhesion-molecule expression make them the site of emigration and of histamine-induced leakage.
**Why not:** A, B, D are not the principal sites.

**Q5.** Fenestrated capillaries are characteristic of:
A) Brain  B) Skeletal muscle  C) Lung alveoli  D) Endocrine glands and gut mucosa

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.2**
**Why D:** Pores permit rapid exchange of water and small solutes, including hormone entry into blood.
**Why not:** A, B, C) Continuous capillaries.

**Q6.** Discontinuous (sinusoidal) capillaries with gaps and absent basal lamina are found in:
A) Liver, spleen, bone marrow  B) Muscle  C) CNS  D) Skin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.2**
**Why A:** They permit passage of plasma proteins and whole cells.
**Why not:** B, C, D have continuous endothelium.

**Q7.** The glomerular capillary is fenestrated but unusual in that its fenestrations:
A) Have thick diaphragms  B) Lack diaphragms  C) Are absent  D) Are intracellular

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 8.2**
**Why B:** Absent diaphragms maximise filtration; selectivity is provided by the GBM and podocyte slit diaphragms.
**Why not:** A) Most other fenestrated capillaries have them. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q8.** Weibel-Palade bodies store:
A) Histamine  B) Renin  C) von Willebrand factor and P-selectin  D) Surfactant

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 8.3**
**Why C:** They allow endothelium to switch rapidly to a prothrombotic, pro-adhesive state.
**Why not:** A) Mast cells/basophils. B) Juxtaglomerular cells. D) Type II pneumocytes.

**Q9.** Endothelial nitric oxide is synthesised from:
A) Histidine  B) Tyrosine  C) Tryptophan  D) L-arginine via eNOS

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.3**
**Why D:** NO causes vasodilation and inhibits platelet aggregation; its loss marks endothelial dysfunction.
**Why not:** A, B, C are precursors of other mediators.

**Q10.** The sequence of leukocyte emigration is:
A) Margination → rolling (selectins) → firm adhesion (integrins/ICAM) → transmigration
B) Adhesion → rolling → margination → transmigration
C) Transmigration → rolling → adhesion
D) Rolling → transmigration → adhesion

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.3**
**Why A:** Selectins mediate low-affinity rolling; integrins mediate firm arrest.
**Why not:** B, C, D misorder the cascade.

**Q11.** The earliest visible lesion of atherosclerosis is the:
A) Fibrous cap  B) Fatty streak of foam cells  C) Calcified plaque  D) Aneurysm

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 8.4**
**Why B:** Macrophages take up oxidised LDL via scavenger receptors to form foam cells.
**Why not:** A) Later. C) Advanced. D) A complication.

**Q12.** Smooth muscle cells in an atheromatous plaque:
A) Remain in the media
B) Undergo apoptosis only
C) Migrate into the intima, proliferate, and synthesise the collagen of the fibrous cap
D) Become endothelium

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.4**
**Why C:** This phenotypic switch from contractile to synthetic builds the cap.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect.

**Q13.** Acute myocardial infarction most often arises from a plaque that is:
A) Severely stenotic and calcified
B) Entirely endothelialised
C) Small and fibrotic
D) Lipid-rich with a thin fibrous cap, prone to rupture

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 8.4**
**Why D:** Composition, not degree of stenosis, determines the acute event — most infarcts follow rupture of non-critically stenotic plaques.
**Why not:** A) Causes stable angina. B, C) Low risk.

**Q14.** Plaques form preferentially at arterial bifurcations because:
A) Disturbed, low laminar shear stress removes the atheroprotective endothelial phenotype
B) Pressure is highest there
C) There is no endothelium
D) Cholesterol is denser there

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 8.4**
**Why A:** Laminar shear normally maintains protective gene expression.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q15.** Vasa vasorum are found in:
A) Capillaries  B) The adventitia and outer media of large vessels  C) The intima only  D) Arterioles

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.1**
**Why B:** The outer wall is too thick to be nourished by luminal diffusion.
**Why not:** A, D) Too small to need them. C) Nourished by the lumen.

**Q16.** Veins possess valves formed by folds of the:
A) Media  B) Adventitia  C) Intima  D) External elastic lamina

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.1**
**Why C:** Intimal folds prevent retrograde flow in low-pressure vessels.
**Why not:** A, B, D do not form valve cusps.

**Q17.** Approximately what proportion of the blood volume is held in the venous system?
A) 10%  B) 30%  C) 95%  D) 70%

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.1**
**Why D:** Veins are the capacitance reservoir.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q18.** Pericytes are:
A) Contractile perivascular cells sharing the capillary basal lamina  B) Endothelial cells  C) Macrophages  D) Fibroblasts of the adventitia

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.2**
**Why A:** They regulate capillary flow and contribute to repair and angiogenesis.
**Why not:** B, C, D are distinct cell types.

**Q19.** The cardiac skeleton electrically insulates atria from ventricles, which means:
A) Conduction is impossible
B) The AV node is the only normal conduction route, imposing the necessary delay
C) The atria contract after the ventricles
D) Purkinje fibres are unnecessary

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 8.6**
**Why B:** This anatomical fact underlies the physiology of AV delay.
**Why not:** A) Conduction proceeds via the AV node. C) Reversed. D) They are essential.

**Q20.** Purkinje fibres are:
A) Nerve fibres  B) Collagen bundles  C) Modified cardiac muscle cells, pale and glycogen-rich, in the subendocardium  D) Smooth muscle

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.6**
**Why C:** "Fibre" here does not mean nerve — a classic trap.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect.

**Q21.** The epicardium is equivalent to the:
A) Parietal pericardium  B) Myocardium  C) Endocardium  D) Visceral pericardium — mesothelium over connective tissue

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 8.6**
**Why D:** It also carries the coronary vessels in adipose tissue.
**Why not:** A) The outer sac. B, C) Inner layers.

**Q22.** Lymphatic capillaries differ from blood capillaries by:
A) Blind-ended origin, overlapping endothelium, anchoring filaments, and an incomplete basal lamina
B) Having a complete basal lamina
C) Containing erythrocytes
D) Having a thick media

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.7**
**Why A:** This construction permits entry of interstitial fluid and large molecules.
**Why not:** B) Incomplete. C) Lymph is typically acellular apart from lymphocytes. D) None.

**Q23.** In the CNS, the blood-brain barrier depends principally on:
A) Fenestrations  B) Continuous endothelium with extensive tight junctions  C) Sinusoids  D) Pericyte absence

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 8.2**
**Why B:** Tight junctions restrict paracellular passage, maintaining a controlled neuronal environment.
**Why not:** A, C) Would defeat the barrier. D) Pericytes are present and contributory.

**Q24.** Endothelium maintains an antithrombotic surface through all EXCEPT:
A) Thrombomodulin  B) Prostacyclin  C) Exposed subendothelial collagen  D) Nitric oxide

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.3**
**Why C:** Exposure of collagen is precisely the *prothrombotic* trigger following injury.
**Why not:** A, B, D are all anticoagulant/antiplatelet mechanisms.

**Q25.** A vessel with a thick media of smooth muscle and a prominent, clearly visible internal elastic lamina, about 2 mm in diameter, is a:
A) Elastic artery  B) Vein  C) Arteriole  D) Muscular artery

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 8.5**
**Why D:** Muscular (distributing) arteries have the most conspicuous IEL.
**Why not:** A) Elastic lamellae throughout the media. C) Only 1–3 muscle layers. B) Thin wall, no prominent IEL.
