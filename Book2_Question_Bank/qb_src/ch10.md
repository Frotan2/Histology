# Chapter 10 — Hemopoiesis

**Q1.** The last mitotically capable stage of erythroid maturation is the:
A) Proerythroblast  B) Polychromatophilic erythroblast  C) Basophilic erythroblast  D) Orthochromatophilic erythroblast

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.2**
**Why B:** Beyond this stage the nucleus is too condensed to divide.
**Why not:** A, C) Earlier dividing stages. D) Post-mitotic, about to extrude its nucleus.

**Q2.** As a haemopoietic cell matures, the general rule is that:
A) Cell size increases and nucleoli appear
B) Cytoplasm becomes more basophilic
C) Cell size decreases, chromatin condenses, nucleoli disappear, N:C ratio falls
D) The nucleus enlarges

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 10.2**
**Why C:** This single rule allows reconstruction of every lineage.
**Why not:** A, B, D describe the reverse direction.

**Q3.** The reticulocyte is identified by supravital staining with new methylene blue because it contains:
A) Residual DNA  B) Denatured haemoglobin  C) Iron granules  D) Residual ribosomal RNA

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.2**
**Why D:** The precipitated RNA forms the reticulum that gives the cell its name.
**Why not:** A) The nucleus is already extruded. C) Siderocytes. B) Heinz bodies.

**Q4.** A raised reticulocyte count in an anaemic patient indicates:
A) An appropriate marrow response, as in haemolysis or blood loss  B) Marrow failure  C) Iron deficiency  D) Aplastic anaemia

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.2**
**Why A:** It shows the marrow can respond when substrate and stimulus are present.
**Why not:** B, C, D would show an inappropriately low count.

**Q5.** In granulopoiesis, the last stage capable of mitosis is the:
A) Myeloblast  B) Myelocyte  C) Promyelocyte  D) Metamyelocyte

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.3**
**Why B:** Specific granules appear at this stage, which is also the final dividing one.
**Why not:** A, C) Earlier dividing stages. D) Post-mitotic with an indented nucleus.

**Q6.** Primary (azurophilic) granules first appear at the stage of the:
A) Myeloblast  B) Myelocyte  C) Promyelocyte  D) Band cell

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.3**
**Why C:** They are lysosomes containing myeloperoxidase, elastase, and defensins.
**Why not:** A) No granules yet. B) Specific granules. D) Too late.

**Q7.** Lineage-specific (neutrophil/eosinophil/basophil) identity becomes morphologically apparent with the appearance of:
A) Primary granules  B) The nuclear indentation  C) Nucleoli  D) Secondary (specific) granules

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.3**
**Why D:** Specific granules define the lineage.
**Why not:** A) Common to all. B, C) Maturation markers, not lineage markers.

**Q8.** Erythropoietin is produced by the:
A) Kidney peritubular interstitial cells in response to hypoxia  B) Liver  C) Bone marrow stroma  D) Spleen

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 10.4**
**Why A:** This explains the anaemia of chronic kidney disease and the use of recombinant EPO.
**Why not:** B) Produces thrombopoietin (and fetal EPO). C, D) Not the main source.

**Q9.** Thrombopoietin is produced mainly by the:
A) Kidney  B) Liver  C) Spleen  D) Thymus

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.4**
**Why B:** Constitutive hepatic production regulates megakaryocyte maturation.
**Why not:** A) EPO. C, D) Not significant sources.

**Q10.** Megakaryocytes release platelets by:
A) Complete cell fragmentation in the sinusoidal lumen
B) Apoptosis in the cords
C) Extending proplatelet processes through sinusoidal endothelium and shedding platelets into the blood
D) Budding into lymphatics

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 10.3**
**Why C:** Their position against the sinusoid wall is functionally essential.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect mechanisms.

**Q11.** Erythroblastic islands are organised around a central:
A) Megakaryocyte  B) Endothelial cell  C) Fibroblast  D) Macrophage supplying iron and phagocytosing extruded nuclei

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 10.3**
**Why D:** The "nurse cell" arrangement couples iron handling to erythropoiesis.
**Why not:** A, B, C do not serve this role.

**Q12.** Only mature cells normally enter the blood from marrow because:
A) Sinusoidal endothelium selects by deformability and surface receptors
B) Immature cells are destroyed
C) There is a basement membrane barrier
D) Immature cells lack haemoglobin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 10.5**
**Why A:** The barrier is selective by maturity, which is why circulating blasts are pathological.
**Why not:** B) Not destroyed. C) The basal lamina is deficient. D) Irrelevant to transit.

**Q13.** In the adult, active red marrow is found principally in the:
A) Long bones of the limbs
B) Vertebrae, sternum, ribs, pelvis, skull, proximal femur and humerus
C) Phalanges
D) Skull only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 10.5**
**Why B:** Hence the posterior iliac crest as the standard biopsy site.
**Why not:** A, C) Converted to yellow marrow. D) Incomplete.

**Q14.** Extramedullary haemopoiesis in chronic severe anaemia occurs in the:
A) Kidney and lung  B) Brain  C) Liver and spleen  D) Skin

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.5**
**Why C:** The fetal sites resume activity, producing hepatosplenomegaly.
**Why not:** A, B, D are not haemopoietic sites.

**Q15.** The "hair-on-end" skull appearance in severe thalassaemia results from:
A) Infection  B) Calcification  C) Tumour  D) Marrow cavity expansion thinning the cortical bone

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 10.5**
**Why D:** Massive erythroid hyperplasia expands the diploë — a histological cause of a radiological sign.
**Why not:** A, B, C are unrelated.

**Q16.** The normal adult myeloid-to-erythroid ratio is approximately:
A) 2:1 to 4:1  B) 1:1  C) 1:3  D) 10:1

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.5**
**Why A:** Granulocytes live hours to days; erythrocytes last 120 days, so more granulocytic precursors are required.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q17.** A useful estimate of normal adult marrow cellularity is:
A) Always 100%  B) Approximately 100 minus the patient's age  C) Always 50%  D) Age plus 20

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.6**
**Why B:** Marrow becomes progressively fattier with age.
**Why not:** A, C, D do not account for age.

**Q18.** The haemopoietic stem cell is characterised by:
A) Lineage commitment  B) Inability to divide  C) Self-renewal plus multilineage differentiation capacity, CD34+  D) Restriction to the myeloid series

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.1**
**Why C:** Self-renewal is the defining property.
**Why not:** A, D) Progenitors. B) False.

**Q19.** Common lymphoid progenitors give rise to:
A) Erythrocytes and platelets  B) Monocytes  C) Neutrophils  D) B, T and NK cells

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 10.1**
**Why D:** CMP yields the myeloid lineages; CLP the lymphoid.
**Why not:** A, B, C derive from the common myeloid progenitor.

**Q20.** G-CSF is used clinically to:
A) Accelerate neutrophil recovery after chemotherapy  B) Raise platelet count  C) Treat anaemia  D) Suppress immunity

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 10.4**
**Why A:** Filgrastim shortens the period of neutropenia.
**Why not:** B) TPO agonists. C) EPO. D) Not immunosuppressive.

**Q21.** Fetal haemopoiesis occurs in the following sequence:
A) Bone marrow → liver → yolk sac
B) Yolk sac → liver (and spleen) → bone marrow
C) Liver → yolk sac → spleen
D) Spleen → yolk sac → marrow

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.5**
**Why B:** Marrow becomes dominant from about 20 weeks onward.
**Why not:** A, C, D misorder the sequence.

**Q22.** The megakaryocyte is recognised in marrow by:
A) A small round nucleus
B) Multiple separate nuclei
C) Very large size with a single enormous multilobed convoluted nucleus
D) Bilobed nucleus with red granules

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 10.6**
**Why C:** Endomitosis produces polyploidy (up to 64N) within one nucleus.
**Why not:** A) Too small. B) It is one polyploid nucleus, not many. D) Eosinophil.

**Q23.** Absence of megakaryocytes together with loss of all lineages in a markedly hypocellular marrow suggests:
A) Immune thrombocytopenia  B) Iron deficiency  C) Myelodysplasia  D) Aplastic anaemia

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.6**
**Why D:** Trilineage failure with fatty replacement.
**Why not:** A) Megakaryocytes are *increased*. C) Dysplastic but usually cellular. B) Erythroid changes only.

**Q24.** In immune thrombocytopenia the marrow typically shows:
A) Normal or increased megakaryocytes  B) Absent megakaryocytes  C) Fibrosis  D) Blasts

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 10.6**
**Why A:** The defect is peripheral destruction, so the marrow compensates.
**Why not:** B) Aplasia. C) Myelofibrosis. D) Leukaemia.

**Q25.** Reconversion of yellow marrow to red marrow occurs in:
A) Normal ageing  B) Chronic severe anaemia or increased haemopoietic demand  C) Obesity  D) Dehydration

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 10.5**
**Why B:** The process is reversible under sustained stimulus.
**Why not:** A) Drives the opposite change. C, D) Unrelated.
