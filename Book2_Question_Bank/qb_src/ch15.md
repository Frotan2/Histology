# Chapter 15 — Skin

**Q1.** The correct order of epidermal layers from base to surface is:
A) Corneum, lucidum, granulosum, spinosum, basale
B) Basale, granulosum, spinosum, corneum
C) Basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum, corneum
D) Spinosum, basale, granulosum, corneum

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.1**
**Why C:** Cells are produced basally and differentiate upward over roughly four weeks.
**Why not:** A) Reversed. B, D) Misordered.

**Q2.** Keratohyalin granules contain filaggrin, which functions to:
A) Cross-link lipid
B) Produce melanin
C) Form desmosomes
D) Aggregate keratin filaments into dense bundles

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.2**
**Why D:** This collapses the cell into a flattened, mechanically tough corneocyte.
**Why not:** A) Lamellar body lipids. B, C) Other structures.

**Q3.** Lamellar bodies discharge their contents in the stratum granulosum to:
A) Form the intercellular lipid "mortar" preventing water loss
B) Produce keratin
C) Create desmosomes
D) Transfer melanin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.2**
**Why A:** The barrier is a brick-and-mortar structure; the lipid provides waterproofing.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q4.** Filaggrin (FLG) loss-of-function mutations are the strongest known genetic risk factor for:
A) Psoriasis  B) Atopic dermatitis (and cause ichthyosis vulgaris)  C) Melanoma  D) Pemphigus

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.3**
**Why B:** A defective barrier allows allergen entry and sensitisation — the "outside-in" hypothesis.
**Why not:** A, C, D have different mechanisms.

**Q5.** Parakeratosis in psoriasis means:
A) Absent keratin
B) Thickened granular layer
C) Retention of nuclei in the stratum corneum
D) Increased melanin

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.3**
**Why C:** Accelerated transit (~4 days) means cells reach the surface before losing their nuclei.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect; the granular layer is *lost*.

**Q6.** The Auspitz sign (pinpoint bleeding on scale removal) in psoriasis results from:
A) Vasculitis  B) Infection  C) Platelet defect  D) Dilated capillaries in elongated dermal papillae close to a thinned suprapapillary plate

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.3**
**Why D:** A clinical sign read directly from the histology.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q7.** Melanocytes are derived from:
A) Neural crest  B) Mesoderm  C) Endoderm  D) Bone marrow

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.4**
**Why A:** They migrate to the basal epidermis during development.
**Why not:** B, C) Wrong germ layer. D) Langerhans cells.

**Q8.** Racial variation in skin colour is due mainly to differences in:
A) Melanocyte number
B) Melanosome size, melanisation, dispersion and degradation rate
C) Keratinocyte number
D) Epidermal thickness

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.4**
**Why B:** Melanocyte density is essentially the same across populations.
**Why not:** A) A common misconception. C, D) Not the basis.

**Q9.** Albinism differs from vitiligo in that albinism features:
A) Absent melanocytes
B) Autoimmune destruction
C) Normal melanocyte number with defective tyrosinase or melanosome biogenesis
D) Patchy depigmentation only

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.4**
**Why C:** Number versus function is the discriminator; vitiligo shows absent melanocytes in affected patches.
**Why not:** A, B, D describe vitiligo.

**Q10.** Melanin protects DNA by accumulating in keratinocytes:
A) Randomly in the cytoplasm
B) In lysosomes only
C) In the nucleus
D) As a cap over the nucleus on the UV-facing side

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.4**
**Why D:** A UV shield positioned precisely where DNA needs protection.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q11.** Langerhans cells are:
A) Bone-marrow-derived antigen-presenting dendritic cells with Birbeck granules
B) Melanin-producing
C) Mechanoreceptors
D) Keratinocyte precursors

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.4**
**Why A:** Located mainly in the stratum spinosum; central to contact dermatitis.
**Why not:** B) Melanocytes. C) Merkel cells. D) Basal keratinocytes.

**Q12.** Merkel cells function as:
A) Immune cells  B) Mechanoreceptors for sustained light touch and texture  C) Pigment cells  D) Secretory cells

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.4**
**Why B:** They associate with an expanded nerve terminal in the basal layer.
**Why not:** A) Langerhans. C) Melanocytes. D) Glandular cells.

**Q13.** Thick skin is distinguished from thin skin by:
A) A thicker dermis
B) More melanocytes
C) A stratum lucidum, very thick corneum, and absence of hair follicles and sebaceous glands
D) Presence of apocrine glands

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.5**
**Why C:** "Thick/thin" refers to *epidermal* thickness — the dermis of the back is thicker than that of the palm.
**Why not:** A) A common trap. B, D) Incorrect.

**Q14.** The Pacinian corpuscle is recognised by:
A) Stacked flattened cells in a dermal papilla
B) A spindle shape
C) Free nerve endings
D) Large oval body of concentric onion-like lamellae in the deep dermis/hypodermis

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.6**
**Why D:** The lamellae filter out sustained pressure, making it a vibration detector.
**Why not:** A) Meissner corpuscle. C) Nociceptors. B) Ruffini.

**Q15.** Meissner corpuscles are located in the:
A) Dermal papillae  B) Hypodermis  C) Stratum corneum  D) Muscle

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.6**
**Why A:** Positioned superficially for light touch and low-frequency vibration.
**Why not:** B) Pacinian. C) Dead layer. D) Muscle spindles.

**Q16.** Eccrine sweat gland ducts are identified by their:
A) Single layer of columnar cells
B) Distinctive two-layered cuboidal epithelium
C) Stratified squamous lining
D) Pseudostratified lining

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.7**
**Why B:** The double-layered duct is the decisive recognition feature.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q17.** The sweat test in cystic fibrosis is abnormal because of failure of:
A) Sweat production
B) Myoepithelial contraction
C) Ductal chloride reabsorption
D) Sodium secretion in the coil

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.7**
**Why C:** Defective CFTR prevents Cl⁻ reabsorption, so sweat chloride is high.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect.

**Q18.** Apocrine glands differ from eccrine glands by:
A) Being distributed over the whole body
B) Lacking myoepithelial cells
C) Producing watery sweat for thermoregulation
D) Opening into hair follicles, being restricted to axilla/areola/anogenital areas, and developing at puberty

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.7**
**Why D:** Their secretion is odourless until bacterial action.
**Why not:** A, C) Eccrine. B) Present.

**Q19.** Sebaceous glands secrete by which mechanism?
A) Holocrine  B) Apocrine  C) Merocrine  D) Endocrine

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.7**
**Why A:** The entire cell disintegrates to become sebum.
**Why not:** C) Exocytosis. B) Apical pinching. D) No duct.

**Q20.** The papillary dermis differs from the reticular dermis by having:
A) Thick collagen bundles
B) Loose connective tissue with fine collagen, capillary loops and Meissner corpuscles
C) No vessels
D) Only elastic fibres

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.8**
**Why B:** Interdigitation with rete ridges increases adhesion and resists shear.
**Why not:** A) Reticular dermis. C, D) Incorrect.

**Q21.** The epidermis is nourished by:
A) Its own capillaries
B) Lymphatics within the epidermis
C) Diffusion from capillaries in the papillary dermis
D) Sweat

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.8**
**Why C:** Epidermis, like all epithelium, is avascular.
**Why not:** A, B) No vessels or lymphatics within it. D) Not nutritive.

**Q22.** Skin is distinguished from oesophageal mucosa (both stratified squamous) by:
A) Cell shape
B) Basement membrane
C) Nuclear size
D) Keratinised anucleate surface plus dermal appendages

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.9**
**Why D:** The oesophagus is non-keratinised with nuclei to the surface, has no appendages, and has a muscularis mucosae.
**Why not:** A, B, C are not discriminating.

**Q23.** Melanocytes appear as clear cells in the basal layer on H&E because of:
A) A shrinkage artifact producing a perinuclear halo  B) Lipid content  C) Glycogen  D) Mucin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.4**
**Why A:** B useful artifact — it makes them findable.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q24.** Acne results from:
A) Eccrine gland blockage
B) Sebaceous follicle obstruction with Cutibacterium acnes colonisation under androgenic stimulation
C) Apocrine infection
D) Melanocyte dysfunction

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.7**
**Why B:** Explains its onset at puberty and follicular distribution.
**Why not:** A, C, D are incorrect.

**Q25.** Desmosomes are most prominent in which epidermal layer, giving it its name?
A) Basale  B) Granulosum  C) Spinosum  D) Corneum

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.1**
**Why C:** The "prickles" are desmosomal contacts persisting where processing shrinkage separates cells.
**Why not:** A) Hemidesmosomes predominate. B, D) Later differentiation stages.
