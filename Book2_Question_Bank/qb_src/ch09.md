# Chapter 9 — Blood

**Q1.** Central pallor of the erythrocyte occupies normally about:
A) One-third of the diameter  B) One-tenth of the diameter  C) Two-thirds  D) None

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.1**
**Why A:** The biconcave shape makes the centre thinner, so less haemoglobin lies in the light path.
**Why not:** B, C, D indicate pathology or misreading.

**Q2.** Hypochromia with enlarged central pallor is typical of:
A) Spherocytosis  B) Iron deficiency and thalassaemia  C) Megaloblastic anaemia  D) Anaemia of chronic disease only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.2**
**Why B:** Reduced haemoglobin content per cell increases the pale zone.
**Why not:** A) Loss of pallor. C) Macro-ovalocytes. D) Usually normochromic.

**Q3.** Spherocytes lack central pallor because:
A) They contain excess haemoglobin
B) They are immature
C) Membrane loss has abolished the biconcave shape
D) They are larger

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.2**
**Why C:** Loss of surface area relative to volume yields a sphere, which is uniformly thick.
**Why not:** A) Haemoglobin content is not increased overall. B) Not immaturity. D) They are smaller in diameter.

**Q4.** Target cells arise when:
A) Haemoglobin content exceeds membrane
B) Iron is excessive
C) The cell is dehydrated
D) The ratio of membrane surface area to haemoglobin content is increased

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 9.2**
**Why D:** Excess membrane relative to contents allows a bell shape that appears as a central dot in a ring of pallor — hence their presence in thalassaemia, iron deficiency, and liver disease.
**Why not:** A) Reversed. B, C) Not the mechanism.

**Q5.** The most numerous leukocyte in normal adult blood is the:
A) Neutrophil  B) Lymphocyte  C) Monocyte  D) Eosinophil

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.3**
**Why A:** 50–70%, ahead of lymphocytes (20–40%).
**Why not:** B) Second. C, D) Minor populations.

**Q6.** The largest circulating leukocyte, with an indented nucleus and grey-blue cytoplasm, is the:
A) Neutrophil  B) Monocyte  C) Large lymphocyte  D) Basophil

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.3**
**Why B:** Kidney/horseshoe nucleus with lacy chromatin and often vacuoles.
**Why not:** A) Multilobed. C) Round dense nucleus, deeply basophilic cytoplasm. D) Obscured by dark granules.

**Q7.** Hypersegmented neutrophils (>5 lobes) suggest:
A) Bacterial infection  B) Allergy  C) Megaloblastic anaemia  D) Chronic myeloid leukaemia

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.4**
**Why C:** Impaired DNA synthesis from B12/folate deficiency.
**Why not:** A) Left shift with band forms. B) Eosinophilia. D) Left shift with basophilia.

**Q8.** Eosinophilia is classically associated with:
A) Pyogenic bacterial infection
B) Aplastic anaemia
C) Viral hepatitis
D) Helminth (parasitic worm) infection, allergy, and drug reactions

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.4**
**Why D:** Major basic protein kills helminths extracellularly, as they are too large to phagocytose.
**Why not:** A) Neutrophilia. C) Lymphocytosis. B) Pancytopenia.

**Q9.** Chronic granulomatous disease results from a defect in:
A) NADPH oxidase  B) Myeloperoxidase only  C) Lysozyme  D) Defensins

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 9.4**
**Why A:** No respiratory burst, so catalase-positive organisms survive; diagnosed by dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry (historically NBT).
**Why not:** B) B separate, milder deficiency. C, D) Not the primary defect.

**Q10.** Basophils and mast cells are best distinguished by the fact that:
A) They are the same cell
B) Basophils circulate and are terminally differentiated; mast cells mature in tissue and are long-lived
C) Only mast cells bind IgE
D) Only basophils contain histamine

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 9.3**
**Why B:** Separate lineage trajectories despite similar granule contents.
**Why not:** A) Distinct cells. C, D) Both bind IgE and contain histamine.

**Q11.** B, T, and NK lymphocytes are distinguished by:
A) Size on a smear  B) Nuclear shape  C) Immunophenotyping (CD19/20, CD3, CD56)  D) Granule colour

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.3**
**Why C:** They are morphologically indistinguishable — an important limit of morphology.
**Why not:** A, B, D cannot reliably separate them.

**Q12.** Atypical lymphocytes of infectious mononucleosis are characteristically:
A) Small with scant cytoplasm
B) Granular and red
C) Multilobed
D) Large with abundant basophilic cytoplasm indented by adjacent red cells

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.4**
**Why D:** These are reactive CD8+ T cells responding to EBV-infected D cells.
**Why not:** A) Normal small lymphocyte. C) Neutrophil. B) Eosinophil.

**Q13.** Platelets are:
A) Anucleate cytoplasmic fragments of megakaryocytes
B) Small nucleated cells
C) Erythrocyte fragments
D) Lymphocyte derivatives

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.5**
**Why A:** 2–4 µm, with a central granulomere and peripheral hyalomere.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q14.** Platelet α-granules contain:
A) ADP and serotonin  B) Fibrinogen, von Willebrand factor and PDGF  C) Histamine  D) Myeloperoxidase

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 9.5**
**Why B:** Dense granules hold ADP, ATP, calcium, and serotonin.
**Why not:** A) Dense granules. C) Mast cells. D) Neutrophils.

**Q15.** Heinz bodies and bite cells indicate:
A) Iron deficiency  B) Sickle cell disease  C) G6PD deficiency with oxidative haemolysis  D) Thalassaemia

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 9.6**
**Why C:** Denatured haemoglobin precipitates and is removed by splenic macrophages, leaving bite-shaped defects.
**Why not:** A) Pencil cells. B) Sickle forms. D) Target cells.

**Q16.** Hereditary spherocytosis most often involves defects of:
A) Haemoglobin  B) Pyruvate kinase  C) G6PD  D) Spectrin, ankyrin or band 3 of the membrane skeleton

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.6**
**Why D:** Vertical linkage failure causes membrane loss and sphering.
**Why not:** A) Haemoglobinopathies. B, C) Enzymopathies.

**Q17.** Splenectomy benefits hereditary spherocytosis because:
A) It removes the site where rigid spherocytes are trapped and destroyed
B) It increases red cell production
C) It corrects the membrane defect
D) It raises erythropoietin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.6**
**Why A:** The cells remain spherocytic but survive longer.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q18.** Howell-Jolly bodies in the peripheral blood indicate:
A) Iron overload  B) Absent or non-functioning spleen  C) Lead poisoning  D) Active infection

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.6**
**Why B:** Nuclear remnants normally "pitted" out by splenic macrophages persist.
**Why not:** A) Siderocytes. C) Basophilic stippling. D) Toxic granulation.

**Q19.** The erythrocyte depends for ATP on:
A) Oxidative phosphorylation  B) β-oxidation  C) Anaerobic glycolysis  D) Ketone bodies

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.1**
**Why C:** It has no mitochondria; the hexose monophosphate shunt supplies NADPH for antioxidant defence.
**Why not:** A, B, D all require mitochondria.

**Q20.** The Mentzer index (MCV ÷ RBC count) below 13 favours:
A) Iron deficiency  B) Aplastic anaemia  C) Megaloblastic anaemia  D) Thalassaemia trait

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 9.7**
**Why D:** Thalassaemia trait shows a preserved/high red cell count with disproportionately low MCV.
**Why not:** A) Index above 13. B, C) Not microcytic.

**Q21.** A "left shift" refers to:
A) Increased band forms and immature granulocytes  B) Hypersegmentation  C) Lymphocytosis  D) Reticulocytosis

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.4**
**Why A:** Reflects accelerated marrow release, typically in bacterial infection.
**Why not:** B) Megaloblastic. C) Viral. D) Erythroid response.

**Q22.** Polychromasia on a Romanowsky-stained film corresponds to:
A) Spherocytes  B) Reticulocytes  C) Target cells  D) Schistocytes

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.7**
**Why B:** Residual ribosomal RNA imparts a blue-grey tinge to these slightly large cells.
**Why not:** A, C, D have different mechanisms and appearances.

**Q23.** Schistocytes (fragmented red cells) suggest:
A) Nutritional deficiency  B) Thalassaemia  C) Microangiopathic haemolysis (e.g. DIC, TTP, mechanical valve)  D) Spherocytosis

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.6**
**Why C:** Mechanical shearing across fibrin strands or prosthetic surfaces.
**Why not:** A, B, D produce different morphologies.

**Q24.** In sickle cell disease, the primary molecular event is:
A) Reduced globin chain synthesis
B) Membrane skeleton defect
C) Oxidation of haemoglobin
D) Polymerisation of deoxygenated HbS into rigid fibres

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 9.6**
**Why D:** Polymerisation deforms the cell, producing haemolysis and vaso-occlusion.
**Why not:** A) Thalassaemia. C) G6PD. B) Spherocytosis.

**Q25.** A small lymphocyte's nucleus is approximately the size of:
A) One erythrocyte  B) A platelet  C) Three erythrocytes  D) A megakaryocyte

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 9.3**
**Why A:** This equivalence is the practical scale reference on any film.
**Why not:** B) Much smaller. C) Would indicate a large cell. D) Vastly larger.
