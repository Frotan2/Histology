# Essential Histology: Question Bank
## A Concept-Based Guide for PGME — Assessment Companion

**Edition:** Essential Histology 1405 — Definitive Edition
**Primary reference:** Mescher AL, *Junqueira's Basic Histology: Text and Atlas*, 17th Edition, McGraw Hill.
**Scope:** The 16 official core chapters of the Afghanistan 1405 Medical Specialty Examination syllabus.

## How to Use This Bank

This bank contains **400 single-best-answer questions — 25 per chapter**, written against the learning objectives of the companion teaching text. Every item carries:

- the **correct answer**;
- an explanation of **why the correct option is right**;
- an explanation of **why each remaining option is wrong**;
- a **difficulty level** (Easy / Medium / Hard);
- the **chapter** and **learning objective** it assesses.

No question tests a fact that is not taught in the textbook. Distractors reproduce the specific confusions addressed by the textbook's "Do not confuse with" sections, so a wrong answer diagnoses a particular misunderstanding rather than merely recording a lost mark.

**Difficulty mix.** Easy items (99) check that a definition or decisive feature has been learned. Medium items (209) require application of a mechanism. Hard items (92) require integration across topics or discrimination between two closely related entities.

**Answer-key balance.** Correct answers are distributed exactly evenly across options A, B, C and D (100 each), so that position gives no clue to the answer.

**A note on evolving science.** A few items address points where the classical textbook answer has been revised by newer evidence — for example the embryological origin of thyroid C cells, and the status of the 30-nm chromatin fibre. These are flagged in their explanations, which state both the current evidence and the older answer that some examinations still expect.

# Chapter 1 — Histology and Methods of Study

**Q1.** In H&E, nuclei stain blue because:
A) Nucleic acid phosphates are anionic and bind the cationic dye haematoxylin
B) Nuclear proteins bind haematoxylin
C) Eosin is a basic dye with affinity for DNA
D) DNA is cationic and binds eosin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.4** (staining chemistry)
**Why A:** Basophilia means affinity for a *basic* (cationic) dye; the phosphate backbone of DNA/RNA is the anion responsible.
**Why not:** B) Proteins are largely cationic and bind eosin. C) Eosin is acidic/anionic. D) Reverses both charges.

**Q2.** Which stain requires a frozen section?
A) Prussian blue  B) Oil Red O  C) PAS  D) Congo red

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.4**
**Why B:** Oil Red O is a lysochrome that partitions into lipid; paraffin processing dissolves lipid in alcohol and xylene, so lipid must be preserved by freezing.
**Why not:** A, C, D all work on paraffin sections — iron, carbohydrate, and amyloid are not extracted by solvents.

**Q3.** A liver biopsy shows magenta cytoplasmic granules with PAS that disappear after diastase pre-treatment. The material is:
A) Mucin  B) Basement membrane  C) Glycogen  D) α1-antitrypsin polymer

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.4**
**Why C:** Diastase (amylase) digests glycogen specifically, so PAS positivity that is *abolished* by diastase identifies glycogen.
**Why not:** A, B, D are all PAS-positive but **diastase-resistant**. α1-antitrypsin globules are the classic PAS-positive diastase-resistant finding.

**Q4.** Von Kossa staining is best described as demonstrating:
A) Calcium directly by chelation
B) Acidic mucins
C) Collagen fibres
D) Phosphate and carbonate anions, with calcium inferred indirectly

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.4**
**Why D:** Silver replaces the cation bound to phosphate/carbonate and is reduced to visible metallic silver. Mineralisation is demonstrated; calcium is inferred.
**Why not:** A) Alizarin red detects calcium more directly. C) Trichrome/silver reticulin stains target collagen. B) Alcian blue targets acidic mucins.

**Q5.** The practical resolution limit of the light microscope (~0.2 µm) is set principally by:
A) The wavelength of visible light and numerical aperture  B) Magnification available  C) Lens grinding quality  D) Section thickness

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.5**
**Why A:** The Abbe relationship makes resolution proportional to wavelength and inversely proportional to numerical aperture.
**Why not:** B, C) Beyond the resolution limit, more magnification gives "empty magnification." D) Thickness affects clarity, not the theoretical limit.

**Q6.** Microvilli appear as an unresolved "brush border" by light microscopy because:
A) They are not stained by eosin
B) Their ~0.1 µm width is below the resolution limit
C) They are destroyed during processing
D) They are only present after fixation

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.5**
**Why B:** Individual microvilli are narrower than 0.2 µm and cannot be separated; they sum into a fuzzy band.
**Why not:** A) They are eosinophilic. C) They survive processing. D) They are native structures.

**Q7.** The chief reason immunohistochemistry on formalin-fixed tissue often requires heat-induced epitope retrieval is:
A) Formalin extracts antigens
B) Paraffin binds antibodies
C) Methylene cross-links mask epitopes
D) Formalin lowers tissue pH irreversibly

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.2**
**Why C:** Formaldehyde forms methylene bridges between proteins, sterically concealing antibody binding sites; heat reverses much of this.
**Why not:** A) Antigens are retained, not extracted. B) Paraffin is removed before staining. D) pH effects are not the principal mechanism.

**Q8.** Glutaraldehyde is preferred over formalin for electron microscopy because it:
A) Penetrates tissue faster
B) Preserves lipids without osmium
C) Preserves epitopes better
D) Cross-links more extensively, better preserving ultrastructure

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.2**
**Why D:** Being a dialdehyde it forms more cross-links and stabilises fine structure.
**Why not:** A) It penetrates *poorly*, hence 1 mm blocks. B) Osmium is still needed for lipid/membranes. C) It destroys many epitopes.

**Q9.** Adipose tissue appears as empty vacuoles in routine sections because:
A) Lipid is not fixed by formaldehyde and is dissolved in alcohol and xylene
B) Adipocytes rupture during sectioning
C) Lipid is eosinophilic but very pale
D) Adipocytes contain no cytoplasm

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.2**
**Why A:** Formaldehyde does not fix lipid; processing solvents extract it, leaving the space.
**Why not:** B) The cell outline survives. C) Lipid is not present to stain. D) B thin cytoplasmic rim remains.

**Q10.** A 3 cm specimen fixed whole in formalin for 2 hours shows well-preserved periphery but blurred nuclei and detached epithelium centrally. The explanation is:
A) Over-fixation of the centre
B) Penetration-limited fixation with central autolysis
C) Ice crystal artifact
D) Knife chatter

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.2**
**Why B:** Formalin penetrates about 1 mm/hour; the centre remains unfixed and autolyses. Large specimens must be sliced.
**Why not:** A) Over-fixation causes hardening, not autolysis. C, D) Are technical artifacts with different appearances.

**Q11.** Congo red demonstrating amyloid must be examined under:
A) Phase contrast  B) Dark field  C) Polarised light for apple-green birefringence  D) UV without filters

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.4**
**Why C:** The ordered β-pleated sheet aligns the dye to give apple-green birefringence, which is the specific criterion.
**Why not:** A, B, D do not produce the diagnostic birefringence.

**Q12.** Which is the *correct* statement about PAS and basement membranes?
A) PAS detects type IV collagen directly
B) PAS positivity of basement membrane is diastase-sensitive
C) PAS is a specific collagen stain
D) PAS is positive because of carbohydrate-rich glycoproteins and proteoglycans

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 1.4**
**Why D:** Laminin, perlecan, nidogen and associated glycoproteins carry the 1,2-glycol groups oxidised by periodic acid.
**Why not:** A, C) PAS is a carbohydrate stain, not a collagen stain. B) Basement membrane PAS positivity is diastase-*resistant*.

**Q13.** Transmission electron microscopy differs from light microscopy fundamentally because:
A) Electrons have a far shorter wavelength
B) Its lenses are better ground
C) It uses higher magnification settings
D) It requires thicker sections

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.5**
**Why A:** Wavelength governs resolution; accelerated electrons have wavelengths thousands of times shorter than light.
**Why not:** B, C) Neither overcomes a wavelength limit. D) TEM uses 60–90 nm ultrathin sections.

**Q14.** A surgeon requests an intraoperative margin assessment. The appropriate technique is:
A) Paraffin section with H&E
B) Frozen section
C) Electron microscopy
D) Decalcified section

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.3**
**Why B:** Frozen section gives an answer in minutes, which is the operative requirement.
**Why not:** A) Takes many hours to overnight. C) Far too slow and narrow-field. D) Only for mineralised tissue, and slow.

**Q15.** Which artifact is recognised by the fact that it crosses tissue boundaries without respecting them?
A) Granuloma  B) Necrosis  C) Knife chatter lines  D) Metaplasia

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.6**
**Why C:** Technical artifacts ignore biology; regular parallel lines crossing all structures indicate blade vibration.
**Why not:** A, B, D are biological processes that respect tissue architecture.

**Q16.** A metachromatic reaction (toluidine blue turning purple on mast cell granules) occurs because:
A) The granules are eosinophilic
B) Mast cells contain melanin
C) The dye is oxidised by heparin
D) Densely packed polyanions alter the dye's absorption spectrum

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 1.4**
**Why D:** High-density sulfated glycosaminoglycan (heparin) causes dye molecules to stack, shifting colour.
**Why not:** A) Metachromasia is unrelated to eosin. C) No oxidation occurs. B) Mast cells contain no melanin.

**Q17.** Reticular fibres are demonstrated by silver impregnation because they are:
A) Argyrophilic — silver is deposited and reduced by an external reducing agent
B) Argentaffin — they reduce silver themselves
C) Birefringent
D) Strongly eosinophilic

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 1.4**
**Why A:** Reticular fibre glycoprotein coats bind silver, which requires an added reducer — the definition of argyrophilic.
**Why not:** B) Melanin and some enterochromaffin granules are argentaffin. C) Not the basis of the stain. D) They are nearly invisible in H&E.

**Q18.** A tubular gland cut tangentially through its wall may appear as:
A) A ring with a lumen
B) A solid mass of tissue without a lumen
C) Two parallel walls
D) A single layer of squamous cells

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.6**
**Why B:** A plane that grazes the wall without entering the lumen shows only wall tissue.
**Why not:** A) Transverse section. C) Longitudinal section. D) Describes an epithelium, not a plane effect on a gland.

**Q19.** A cuboidal epithelium may be mistaken for simple squamous when:
A) The stain is too strong
B) The tissue is frozen
C) The section plane passes above or below the level of the nuclei
D) The magnification is too high

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.6**
**Why C:** Without nuclei in the plane, the residual cytoplasm looks like a thin flat layer.
**Why not:** A, B, D do not change apparent cell height in this systematic way.

**Q20.** The decisive test distinguishing pseudostratified from stratified epithelium is:
A) Nuclear height  B) Presence of cilia  C) Number of visible nuclear rows  D) Whether every cell contacts the basement membrane

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.6**
**Why D:** Pseudostratification is by definition a single layer with nuclei at different heights, all cells reaching the base.
**Why not:** A, C) Both are misleading precisely because of nuclear staggering. B) Cilia are common but not definitional.

**Q21.** The erythrocyte is used as an internal scale reference because its diameter is approximately:
A) 7.5 µm  B) 2 µm  C) 15 µm  D) 25 µm

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.5**
**Why A:** ~7.5 µm, conveniently similar to a small lymphocyte nucleus.
**Why not:** B) Platelet scale. C) Monocyte scale. D) Larger than most leukocytes.

**Q22.** Osmium tetroxide is used in EM preparation because it:
A) Dissolves lipid  B) Fixes and stains unsaturated lipids, rendering membranes electron-dense  C) Removes protein  D) Decalcifies tissue

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 1.2**
**Why B:** It reacts with carbon–carbon double bonds in membrane lipids and adds electron density.
**Why not:** A) The opposite. C, D) Not its function.

**Q23.** Masson trichrome is most useful when the clinical question is:
A) Is there iron?  B) Is there amyloid?  C) How much fibrosis is present?  D) Is there glycogen?

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.3**
**Why C:** Trichrome separates collagen (blue/green) from muscle and cytoplasm (red), quantifying fibrosis.
**Why not:** A) Perls. B) Congo red. D) PAS with diastase.

**Q24.** A pathologist must distinguish alcoholic fatty liver from glycogen storage disease on a biopsy where both show pale, swollen hepatocytes. The correct pair of tests is:
A) Congo red and Perls
B) Trichrome and reticulin
C) Von Kossa and Alcian blue
D) Oil Red O on frozen section, and PAS with diastase

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 1.3**
**Why D:** Lipid requires a frozen section with a lysochrome; glycogen is identified by diastase-sensitive PAS positivity. Together they discriminate the two.
**Why not:** A) Amyloid and iron. C) Mineral and acidic mucin. B) Fibrosis and architecture.

**Q25.** In a systematic approach to an unknown slide, the correct first step is to:
A) Use scanning power to establish overall architecture
B) Switch to 40× and identify cells
C) Order special stains
D) Count mitoses

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 1.6**
**Why A:** Architecture constrains the possibilities before any cell is named; cells confirm rather than initiate the diagnosis.
**Why not:** B) Premature high power is the commonest student error. C, D) Come much later, if at all.

# Chapter 2 — Cytoplasm

**Q1.** Intensely basophilic cytoplasm indicates abundant:
A) Mitochondria  B) Rough ER and free ribosomes  C) Smooth ER  D) Lipid droplets

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 2.1**
**Why B:** Ribosomal RNA is the polyanion binding haematoxylin.
**Why not:** A) Mitochondria give eosinophilia. C, D) Give pale, foamy cytoplasm.

**Q2.** A cell with pale, foamy, vacuolated cytoplasm and tubular mitochondrial cristae is most likely secreting:
A) Antibody  B) Digestive enzymes  C) Steroid hormone  D) Mucus

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.3**
**Why C:** Tubular cristae house steroidogenic P450 enzymes; lipid droplets (extracted in processing) hold cholesterol precursor.
**Why not:** A, B) Would be strongly basophilic with RER. D) Mucin granules give a different, apically clustered pale appearance.

**Q3.** The perinuclear "hof" of a plasma cell corresponds to:
A) Nucleolus  B) Centriole pair only  C) Lysosome cluster  D) Golgi apparatus

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 2.2**
**Why D:** The Golgi is large in professional secretory cells but unstained in H&E, appearing as a pale zone.
**Why not:** A) Is intranuclear. C) Would not be uniformly pale. B) Too small to see by light microscopy.

**Q4.** The signal recognition particle functions to:
A) Pause translation and target the ribosome to the RER translocon
B) Cleave the signal peptide
C) Add mannose-6-phosphate
D) Fold nascent protein

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.2**
**Why A:** SRP binds the emerging signal sequence, halts elongation, and docks on the SRP receptor.
**Why not:** B) Signal peptidase does this. C) GlcNAc-phosphotransferase in cis-Golgi. D) Chaperones such as BiP.

**Q5.** In I-cell disease the fundamental defect is failure to:
A) Synthesise lysosomal enzymes
B) Tag lysosomal enzymes with mannose-6-phosphate
C) Fuse lysosomes with phagosomes
D) Acidify the lysosome

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 2.2**
**Why B:** Deficient GlcNAc-phosphotransferase means no M6P sorting signal; enzymes are secreted rather than delivered.
**Why not:** A) Enzymes are made normally — serum levels are *high*. C, D) Not the primary lesion.

**Q6.** The diagnostic biochemical paradox of I-cell disease is:
A) Low serum and low cellular enzyme
B) High cellular enzyme with low serum enzyme
C) High serum enzyme with deficient intracellular enzyme
D) Normal enzyme with abnormal substrate

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 2.2**
**Why C:** Mis-sorted enzymes are secreted into plasma while lysosomes remain enzyme-poor.
**Why not:** A, B, D misstate the sorting defect.

**Q7.** In α1-antitrypsin deficiency, the liver injury results from:
A) Absence of the protein in hepatocytes
B) Autoimmune attack on hepatocytes
C) Excess protease in the liver
D) Retention of misfolded polymer in hepatocyte RER (toxic gain of function)

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 2.2**
**Why D:** PiZ protein polymerises and is retained, producing PAS-positive diastase-resistant globules and hepatocyte injury.
**Why not:** A) That mechanism (loss of function) explains the *lung* disease. B, C) Not operative.

**Q8.** Panacinar emphysema in α1-antitrypsin deficiency occurs because:
A) Elastase destroys alveolar elastin unopposed
B) Collagen is overproduced
C) Surfactant is deficient
D) Mucus plugs the airways

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.2**
**Why A:** Absent circulating antiprotease permits neutrophil elastase to degrade alveolar wall elastin.
**Why not:** B, C, D describe other lung diseases.

**Q9.** Tubular mitochondrial cristae are characteristically seen in:
A) Cardiac myocytes  B) Adrenal cortical cells  C) Plasma cells  D) Neutrophils

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.3**
**Why B:** Steroid-secreting cells show tubulovesicular cristae.
**Why not:** A) Lamellar cristae, very numerous. C, D) Not steroidogenic.

**Q10.** Heteroplasmy explains why mitochondrial diseases:
A) Are inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern
B) Affect only males
C) Show variable severity and threshold effects in high-demand tissues
D) Always present at birth

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 2.3**
**Why C:** A variable proportion of mutant mtDNA per cell means disease appears only above a tissue-specific threshold.
**Why not:** A) Inheritance is maternal. B) Both sexes affected. D) Onset is variable.

**Q11.** Intermediate filament proteins are diagnostically valuable in tumour pathology because they:
A) Are the strongest filaments
B) Are present only in cancer
C) Bind antibodies non-specifically
D) Are lineage-specific and persist despite dedifferentiation

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.4**
**Why D:** Cytokeratin/vimentin/desmin/GFAP identify epithelial, mesenchymal, muscle, and glial lineage respectively.
**Why not:** A) Irrelevant to diagnosis. C) The opposite is required. B) They are normal constituents.

**Q12.** A poorly differentiated metastasis stains positive for cytokeratin. The lineage is:
A) Epithelial (carcinoma)  B) Mesenchymal (sarcoma)  C) Glial  D) Lymphoid

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 2.4**
**Why A:** Cytokeratins are the intermediate filaments of epithelium.
**Why not:** B) Vimentin. C) GFAP. D) CD45/LCA.

**Q13.** The axoneme of a motile cilium has the arrangement:
A) 9+0  B) 9+2  C) 9 triplets  D) 13 protofilaments only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 2.4**
**Why B:** Nine outer doublets plus a central pair.
**Why not:** A) The non-motile primary cilium. C) Centriole/basal body. D) Describes a single microtubule wall.

**Q14.** Kartagener syndrome results from a defect in:
A) Kinesin  B) Actin  C) Dynein arms  D) Lamin A

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.4**
**Why C:** Absent dynein arms prevent doublet sliding and ciliary beating.
**Why not:** A) Anterograde microtubule motor, not the ciliary bending motor here. B, D) Unrelated systems.

**Q15.** Situs inversus occurs in primary ciliary dyskinesia because:
A) Cilia determine organ size
B) Mucus accumulates in the chest
C) Sperm cannot swim
D) Nodal cilia establish embryonic left–right asymmetry

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 2.4**
**Why D:** Without directional nodal flow, laterality is randomised, so ~50% of patients have situs inversus.
**Why not:** A) False. B, C) Are other features of the same syndrome, not the cause of laterality defects.

**Q16.** The primary cilium differs from a motile cilium in that it is:
A) 9+0, solitary and sensory  B) 9+2 and motile  C) Composed of actin  D) Found only in the airway

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.4**
**Why A:** B solitary non-motile sensory antenna present on most cells; dysfunction underlies polycystic kidney disease.
**Why not:** B) Describes motile cilia. C) Microvilli are actin-cored. D) It is near-ubiquitous.

**Q17.** Colchicine and vinca alkaloids arrest mitosis by acting on:
A) Actin  B) Microtubules  C) Intermediate filaments  D) Nuclear lamina

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 2.4**
**Why B:** They prevent tubulin polymerisation, so the spindle cannot form.
**Why not:** A) Cytochalasin/phalloidin. C) No pharmacological agents in routine use. D) Not a drug target here.

**Q18.** The unfolded protein response is triggered by:
A) Excess correctly folded protein in the Golgi
B) Mitochondrial depolarisation
C) Accumulation of misfolded protein in the ER lumen
D) DNA damage

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.2**
**Why C:** Chaperone saturation signals translational attenuation, chaperone upregulation, and ultimately apoptosis.
**Why not:** A) Not a stress. B, D) Trigger other pathways.

**Q19.** Peroxisomes are distinguished from lysosomes by containing:
A) Acid hydrolases  B) Proton pumps only  C) Mannose-6-phosphate receptors  D) Catalase and oxidases for β-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.3**
**Why D:** Peroxisomes generate and then degrade H₂O₂ using catalase and handle VLCFA.
**Why not:** A, C) Lysosomal features. B) Not distinguishing.

**Q20.** Zellweger syndrome is a disorder of:
A) Peroxisome biogenesis  B) Lysosomal enzyme sorting  C) Mitochondrial DNA  D) Ciliary dynein

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 2.3**
**Why A:** PEX gene mutations prevent peroxisome assembly, causing VLCFB accumulation and severe neurological disease.
**Why not:** B) I-cell disease. C) Mitochondrial cytopathies. D) Ciliopathies.

**Q21.** Intensely eosinophilic, granular cytoplasm in the gastric parietal cell reflects:
A) Abundant RER  B) Abundant mitochondria supplying ATP for proton pumping  C) Stored mucin  D) Glycogen

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.1**
**Why B:** Pumping H⁺ against a millionfold gradient is energetically enormous.
**Why not:** A) Would be basophilic. C, D) Are extracted and appear pale.

**Q22.** The microvillus core is composed of:
A) Microtubules  B) Keratin  C) Actin filaments  D) Desmin

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 2.4**
**Why C:** Bundled actin filaments cross-linked by villin and fimbrin, anchored in the terminal web.
**Why not:** A) Cilia. B, D) Intermediate filaments with structural, not projection-forming, roles.

**Q23.** Stereocilia in the epididymis are correctly described as:
A) True motile cilia  B) Keratin projections  C) Microtubule 9+0 structures  D) Long, branching microvilli (actin-based)

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.4**
**Why D:** Despite the name, they are elongated actin-cored microvilli and are non-motile.
**Why not:** A, C) Both imply an axoneme, which they lack. B) Incorrect protein.

**Q24.** An electron micrograph shows a cell with extensive RER, a large Golgi, and abundant secretory granules. The best functional description is:
A) Regulated secretion of protein  B) Steroid secretion  C) Active ion transport  D) Phagocytosis

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.2**
**Why A:** Storage granules indicate a *regulated* (stimulus-dependent) protein secretory pathway.
**Why not:** B) Would show SER and lipid, no granules. C) Would show mitochondria and infoldings. D) Would show lysosomes and phagosomes.

**Q25.** Lipofuscin is best characterised as:
A) An infectious inclusion
B) Indigestible residue of lipid peroxidation accumulating in lysosomes with age
C) Stored glycogen
D) A viral protein aggregate

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 2.5**
**Why B:** The golden-brown "wear-and-tear" pigment of long-lived cells such as neurons, cardiac myocytes, hepatocytes.
**Why not:** A, D) Not infectious. C) Glycogen is PAS-positive and extracted differently.

# Chapter 3 — Nucleus

**Q1.** A large, pale, open nucleus with a prominent nucleolus indicates a cell that is:
A) Quiescent  B) Apoptotic  C) Transcriptionally and synthetically active  D) Terminally keratinised

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 3.1**
**Why C:** Euchromatin is decondensed and accessible; a large nucleolus means active ribosome production.
**Why not:** A) Would be small and dense. B) Pyknotic/fragmented. D) Anucleate.

**Q2.** Heterochromatin appears dark in H&E because it is:
A) Rich in protein only  B) Calcified  C) Bound to eosin  D) Densely packed, concentrating DNA phosphate per unit volume

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.1**
**Why D:** Compaction raises local anionic density, so more haematoxylin binds per unit area.
**Why not:** A) DNA is the key binder. C) It is basophilic. B) Not mineralised.

**Q3.** The number of Barr bodies in a 47,XXY (Klinefelter) cell is:
A) 1  B) 0  C) 2  D) 3

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.2**
**Why A:** Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes minus one.
**Why not:** B) 46,XY or 45,X. C) 47,XXX. D) 48,XXXX.

**Q4.** X-inactivation in females produces mosaicism because it is:
A) Uniform across all cells
B) Random in each cell but clonally inherited thereafter
C) Determined by paternal origin always
D) Reversed at puberty

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.2**
**Why B:** Random early choice, faithfully propagated, creates patches of two cell populations.
**Why not:** A) Would preclude mosaicism. C) Random, not imprinted, in humans generally. D) It is stable.

**Q5.** The current evidence regarding the 30-nm chromatin fibre is best stated as:
A) It is the proven universal structure of interphase chromatin
B) It does not exist under any condition
C) It forms in vitro and in some specialised chromatins, but is not demonstrated as a regular structure in typical interphase nuclei
D) It is composed of microtubules

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 3.1**
**Why C:** Cryo-EM, SAXS and Hi-B favour irregularly folded, interdigitated 10-nm fibres in a disordered state in vivo.
**Why not:** A) Overstates the evidence. B) Understates it — it is demonstrable in vitro and in starfish sperm/avian erythrocytes. D) Wrong polymer entirely.

**Q6.** Nuclear import of a protein bearing an NLS requires:
A) Exportin and Ran-GDP  B) Vesicular transport  C) Passive diffusion only  D) Importin, with cargo release by Ran-GTP in the nucleus

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.3**
**Why D:** Ran-GTP is high in the nucleus (Ran-GEF), causing importin to release cargo there.
**Why not:** A) Describes export. C) Only for molecules under ~40 kDa. B) The envelope is not crossed by vesicles.

**Q7.** The nucleolus is basophilic principally because it contains abundant:
A) rRNA  B) DNA only  C) Lipid  D) Calcium

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 3.4**
**Why A:** rRNB transcription and ribosome assembly concentrate RNB there.
**Why not:** B) DNB contributes, but rRNB dominates. C, D) Not nucleolar constituents.

**Q8.** Nucleolar organiser regions are located on the:
A) Metacentric chromosomes  B) Acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, 22  C) Sex chromosomes  D) Chromosome 1 only

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 3.4**

# Chapter 15 — Skin

**Q1.** The correct order of epidermal layers from base to surface is:
A) Corneum, lucidum, granulosum, spinosum, basale
B) Basale, granulosum, spinosum, corneum

D) Spinosum, basale, granulosum, corneum

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.1**
**Why C:** Cells are produced basally and differentiate upward over roughly four weeks.
**Why not:** A) Reversed. B, D) Misordered.

**Q2.** Keratohyalin granules contain filaggrin, which functions to:
A) Cross-link lipid
B) Produce melanin
C) Form desmosomes
D) Aggregate keratin filaments into dense bundles

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.2**
**Why D:** This collapses the cell into a flattened, mechanically tough corneocyte.
**Why not:** A) Lamellar body lipids. B, C) Other structures.

**Q3.** Lamellar bodies discharge their contents in the stratum granulosum to:
A) Form the intercellular lipid "mortar" preventing water loss
B) Produce keratin
C) Create desmosomes
D) Transfer melanin

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.2**
**Why A:** The barrier is a brick-and-mortar structure; the lipid provides waterproofing.
**Why not:** B, C, D are incorrect.

**Q4.** Filaggrin (FLG) loss-of-function mutations are the strongest known genetic risk factor for:
A) Psoriasis  B) Atopic dermatitis (and cause ichthyosis vulgaris)  C) Melanoma  D) Pemphigus

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.3**
**Why B:** A defective barrier allows allergen entry and sensitisation — the "outside-in" hypothesis.
**Why not:** A, C, D have different mechanisms.

**Q5.** Parakeratosis in psoriasis means:
A) Absent keratin
B) Thickened granular layer
C) Retention of nuclei in the stratum corneum
D) Increased melanin

**Answer: C** · **Difficulty:** Medium · **LO 15.3**
**Why C:** Accelerated transit (~4 days) means cells reach the surface before losing their nuclei.
**Why not:** A, B, D are incorrect; the granular layer is *lost*.

**Q6.** The Auspitz sign (pinpoint bleeding on scale removal) in psoriasis results from:
A) Vasculitis  B) Infection  C) Platelet defect  D) Dilated capillaries in elongated dermal papillae close to a thinned suprapapillary plate

**Answer: D** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.3**
**Why D:** A clinical sign read directly from the histology.
**Why not:** A, B, C are incorrect.

**Q7.** Melanocytes are derived from:

**Answer: A** · **Difficulty:** Easy · **LO 15.4**

**Why not:** B, C) Wrong germ layer. D) Langerhans cells.

**Q8.** Racial variation in skin colour is due mainly to differences in:

**Answer: B** · **Difficulty:** Hard · **LO 15.4**

**Q9.** Albinism differs from vitiligo in that albinism features:

**Q10.** Melanin protects DNA by accumulating in keratinocytes:

**Q11.** Langerhans cells are:

**Q24.** Acne results from:
